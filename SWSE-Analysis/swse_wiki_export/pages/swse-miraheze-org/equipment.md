# Equipment

> Source: [https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment) · Retrieved 2026-10-09T09:36:21Z.

*Reference Book: [Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*

In a galaxy of high-tech wonders, the only limits to what types of Equipment are commercially available depends on the inventors, merchants, and corporations that manufacture and supply them. Most of the items described in this page are available through legitimate arms and equipment traders.

## Weapons

The galaxy is a dangerous place. Most people have access to some kind of weapon, and those who travel the space lanes often carry a blaster or some other weapon as a means of defense. A weapon's legality depends on where you are. No one would look twice at a character wearing a blaster at their side on [Tatooine](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tatooine "Tatooine") or [Nar Shaddaa](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa "Nar Shaddaa"). The same character would do well to conceal it while visiting the gleaming metropolis of [Coruscant](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Coruscant "Coruscant").

When you choose a weapon for your character, refer to your [Weapon Proficiency](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Proficiency "Weapon Proficiency") feat and the links in the [Weapon Groups](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Groups "Weapon Groups") page for details about the various weapon options. The Weapon Qualities that are explained on their respective pages are explained below:

Weapon QualitiesWeapon Qualities

- **Weapon Group:** A weapon's group tells you which [Weapon Proficiency](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Proficiency "Weapon Proficiency") feat you need to be considered proficient with the weapon. If you don't have the appropriate [Weapon Proficiency](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Proficiency "Weapon Proficiency"), you take a -5 penalty on attacks made with the weapon.
- **Size:** The weapon's size (see Weapon Sizes, below).
- **Cost:** The weapon's cost in credits.
- **Damage:** The damage the weapon deals with each hit. Melee Weapons also add the wielder's [Strength](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Strength "Strength") bonus to damage, or twice the wielder's [Strength](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Strength "Strength") bonus when wielded in two hands.
  - [Double Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Double_Weapons "Double Weapons") have two damage entries separated by a slash; these represent the damage dealt by each end of the Double Weapon.
- **[Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun") Setting:** If the weapon has a [Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun") Setting, it is listed here. A weapon set to [Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun") does [Stun Damage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun_Damage "Stun Damage") equal to its normal damage.
  - Ranged Weapons set to [Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun") have a maximum range of 6 squares, unless noted otherwise.
- **Rate of Fire:** Ranged weapons have either a single-shot setting (S) or an [Autofire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Autofire "Autofire") setting (A). A few weapons have both settings and can be set to either single-shot mode or [Autofire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Autofire "Autofire") mode as a [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action"). Only Ranged Weapons that hold multiple shots of ammunition can have an [Autofire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Autofire "Autofire") setting.
  - A weapon can only be on one alternate setting other than its default setting (such as [Autofire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Autofire "Autofire") or [Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun")) at a time. [Autofire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Autofire "Autofire")-only weapons with [Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun") settings can deal [Stun](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun "Stun") damage in [Autofire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Autofire "Autofire") mode, but still have a maximum range of 6 squares, unless noted otherwise.
- **Weight:** The weapon's weight in kilograms.
- **Damage Type:** The type of damage a weapon deals. Some creatures and objects take more or less damage from weapons that deal certain types of damage. Some weapons deal more than one type of damage, depending on how the weapon is used:
  - *And:* The weapon deals both types of damage simultaneously.
  - *Or:* The weapon deals one type of damage or the other, chosen immediately before making the attack roll.
    - [Double Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Double_Weapons "Double Weapons") sometimes deal a different damage type depending on which end of the weapon is used: in this case the two damage types are separated by a slash.
  - *([Ion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ion "Ion")):* Ion Weapons have special rules and effects. See [Ion Damage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ion_Damage "Ion Damage") for more details.
- **Availability:** Some weapons have limited availability, as noted below.
  - *Licensed, Restricted, Military, or Illegal:* Ownership of the weapon is limited to certain individuals as noted in the Restricted Items section below.
  - *Rare:* A Rare weapon is generally available only on its planet of origin (for example, [Naboo](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Naboo "Naboo") for the [Atlatl](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Atlatl "Atlatl") and [Cesta](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Cesta "Cesta"), or [Kashyyyk](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Kashyyyk "Kashyyyk") for the [Bowcaster](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Bowcaster "Bowcaster")) or by acquiring it directly from the manufacturer.

### Weapon Sizes

The size of a weapon compared to your size determines whether the weapon is Light, One-Handed, Two-Handed, or Too Large for you to use.

- **Light:** If the weapon's size is smaller than your size (a [Human](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Human "Human") using a Small weapon such as a [Blaster Pistol](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Blaster_Pistol "Blaster Pistol")), then the weapon is Light. Light Weapons can be used while [Grappling](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grappling "Grappling"), and Light Melee Weapons can be used with the [Weapon Finesse](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Finesse "Weapon Finesse") feat.
- **One-Handed:** If the weapon's size category is the same as your size (a [Human](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Human "Human") using a Medium weapon such as a [Heavy Blaster Pistol](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Blaster_Pistol "Heavy Blaster Pistol")), then the weapon is One-Handed.
- **Two-Handed:** If the weapon's size category is one step larger than your size (a [Human](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Human "Human") using a Large weapon, such as a [Light Repeating Blaster](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Light_Repeating_Blaster "Light Repeating Blaster")), then the weapon requires two hands to use (and sometimes a special mount such as a tripod).
- **Too Large:** If the weapon's size category is two or more steps larger than your size (an [Ewok](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ewok "Ewok") using a Large weapon, such as a [Blaster Cannon](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Blaster_Cannon "Blaster Cannon")), the weapon is Too Large to use. The exceptions to this are [Vehicle Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Weapons "Vehicle Weapons"), which are housed in a unit that aids in their use.

### Melee Weapons

Used in close combat, Melee Weapons usually deal Bludgeoning, Slashing, or Piercing damage, depending on the weapon's design. Some combine powered components to augment the user's own strength. Many types of Melee Weapons are widely available and have few governmental or legal restrictions. Civilians, law enforcement agents, and military personnel alike carry these items. A character's [Strength](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Strength "Strength") modifier is always applied to a Melee Weapon's attack roll and damage roll.

Below are Melee Weapons by [Weapon Groups](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Groups "Weapon Groups"):

- [Advanced Melee Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Advanced_Melee_Weapons "Advanced Melee Weapons")- Technologically enhanced Melee Weapons, primarily using vibration technology.
- [Exotic Weapons (Melee)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Exotic_Weapons_(Melee) "Exotic Weapons (Melee)")- Unusual, or unique Melee Weapons that require specific training to properly utilize.
- [Lightsabers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsabers "Lightsabers")- Swords of pure energy, typically used by [Force Traditions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Traditions "Force Traditions").
- [Simple Weapons (Melee)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Simple_Weapons_(Melee) "Simple Weapons (Melee)")- Basic Melee Weapons that are simple or primitive in nature and use.

### Ranged Weapons

At any given time, hundreds of manufacturers are creating and marketing a wide array of ranged weapons. These weapons run the gamut from small, concealable handheld weapons to tripod-mounted cannons that require a small crew to operate.

Below are Ranged Weapons by [Weapon Groups](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Groups "Weapon Groups"):

- [Exotic Weapons (Ranged)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Exotic_Weapons_(Ranged) "Exotic Weapons (Ranged)")- Unusual, or unique Ranged Weapons that require specific training to properly utilize.
- [Heavy Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Weapons "Heavy Weapons")- Large Ranged Weapons that require both hands, and in some cases a tripod, to accurately use.
- [Pistols](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Pistols "Pistols")- Handheld Ranged Weapons, favored for self-defense, and are easily concealed.
- [Rifles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rifles "Rifles")- Standard military weapons, used with both hands for precise and accurate shooting.
- [Simple Weapons (Ranged)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Simple_Weapons_(Ranged) "Simple Weapons (Ranged)")- Basic Ranged Weapons that are simple or primitive in nature and use.
  - [Grenades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grenades "Grenades")- Highly volatile explosives, generally used against heavily armored, or immobile targets.

### Improvised Weapons

Sometimes objects not crafted to be weapons can be used: chairs, bottles, crates, and so on. Because these objects are not designed for such use, characters who use Improvised Weapons are treated as not proficient with them and take a -5 penalty on their attack rolls. The GM determines the size and damage dealt by an Improvised Weapon.

Sometimes such Improvised Weapons can be [Thrown](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Thrown "Thrown") in a dangerous manner: small rocks, vases, pitchers, and so forth. Because these objects are not designed for such use, characters who use Improvised Thrown Weapons are treated as not proficient with them and take a -5 penalty on their attack rolls.

## Armor

Protective Armor exists in the *Star Wars* universe, but only the lightest types see widespread use. Heavier Armor is considered to be too expensive, too restrictive, and just not worth the trouble unless it serves an additional function (such as providing environmental protection, as in the case of Imperial [Snowtrooper Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Snowtrooper_Armor "Snowtrooper Armor")). Many forms of Armor are restricted or even illegal outside of approved military uses. In locations where Armor isn't prohibited, the wearer of Armor identifies themselves as someone who either expects to cause trouble or expects trouble to come his way.

If you choose to use Armor for your character, refer to the following Armor Qualities for details about their use:

Armor QualitiesArmor Qualities

**Cost:** The cost of the Armor. Armor sized for Small characters cost half as much, while Armor for Large characters costs double the listed price. Some Armor has limited availability (see Rare, below).

**Armor Bonus to Reflex Defense:** When you are wearing the armor, you add this value as an armor bonus to your [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense") (instead of adding your Heroic Level to your [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense")).

**Equipment Bonus to Fortitude Defense:** When you are wearing the armor, you add this value as an Equipment bonus to your [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense"). Some types of Armor do not provide an Equipment bonus to your [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense").

**Maximum Dex Bonus:** This is the maximum bonus you can apply to your [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense") from [Dexterity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dexterity "Dexterity") when wearing this type of Armor. Heavier Armor limits your mobility, reducing your ability to avoid attacks. For example, an [Armored Flight Suit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armored_Flight_Suit "Armored Flight Suit") has a maximum [Dexterity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dexterity "Dexterity") bonus of +3. A character with a [Dexterity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dexterity "Dexterity") of 18 normally gains a +4 bonus to their [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense") from [Dexterity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dexterity "Dexterity"), but if they're wearing an [Armored Flight Suit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armored_Flight_Suit "Armored Flight Suit"), they only apply a +3 bonus to their [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense").

**Speed:** [Medium Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Medium_Armor "Medium Armor") and [Heavy Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Armor "Heavy Armor") reduces your [Speed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Speed "Speed") to three-quarters normal (rounded down). The individual tables per Armor Proficiency show these reductions if you have a speed of 6 squares or 4 squares. In addition, when wearing [Heavy Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Armor "Heavy Armor"), you can only move up to three times your speed when [Running](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Running "Running") (instead of four times your [Speed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Speed "Speed")).

**Weight:** The weight of the Armor. Armor fitted for Small characters weighs half as much. Armor fitted for Large characters weighs twice as much.

**Availability:** Some armor has limited availability, as indicated below.

- **Rare:** This armor is generally only available by going to its planet of origin or by acquiring it directly from its wearer (for example, getting [Vonduun Crabshell](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vonduun_Crabshell "Vonduun Crabshell") armor from a [Yuuzhan Vong](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Yuuzhan_Vong "Yuuzhan Vong") warrior). When available on the open market elsewhere, these items usually cost double the listed price, sometimes much more.
- **Licensed, Restricted, Military, or Illegal:** Ownership of the armor is limited to certain people, as described in Restricted Items, below.

List of armors by Proficiency:

- **[Light Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Light_Armor "Light Armor"):** The most common Armors, found anywhere from civilians to experienced hunters.
- **[Medium Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Medium_Armor "Medium Armor"):** Considered the standard other Armors compare themselves to, these Armors are typically used by military troopers, and various militia.
- **[Heavy Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Armor "Heavy Armor"):** Top of the line Armor, primarily used by military specialists, and high-spec mercenaries.

A character's [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense") can be improved with Armor; however a character who wears armor chooses to apply the armor's bonus to [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense") instead of their Heroic Level. They also choose to take a penalty on certain [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks"), as noted below.

As a character gains levels, Armor bonuses become less enticing. For example, a 5th-level heroic character has less reason to wear Armor that grants an Armor bonus of +5 or less, unless the Armor confers some other useful benefit or the character has the **[Armored Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armored_Defense "Armored Defense")** or **[Improved Armored Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Improved_Armored_Defense "Improved Armored Defense")** Talents. Still, some characters enjoy the benefit of having a higher [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense") and [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"); against very dangerous opponents, Armor can literally be the difference between life and death.

### Armor Check Penalty

While wearing armor with which you are not proficient, you take an armor check penalty on attack rolls, as well as [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") made using the following [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills"):

- [Acrobatics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Acrobatics "Acrobatics")
- [Climb](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Climb "Climb")
- [Endurance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Endurance "Endurance")
- [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative")
- [Jump](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jump "Jump")
- [Stealth](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stealth "Stealth")
- [Swim](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swim "Swim")

The type of Armor worn determines the size of the penalty: [Light Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Light_Armor "Light Armor"), -2; [Medium Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Medium_Armor "Medium Armor"), -5; [Heavy Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Armor "Heavy Armor"), -10. Additionally, you do not gain the armor's Equipment bonuses.

For example, a character not proficient with [Light Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Light_Armor "Light Armor") who dons a suit of [Stormtrooper Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stormtrooper_Armor "Stormtrooper Armor") takes a -2 penalty on attack rolls and certain [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") (see above). In addition, they do not gain the Armor's Equipment bonus on [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") checks, and cannot apply the Armor's Equipment bonus to his [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense").

## [General Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/General_Equipment "General Equipment")

Weapons and Armor are not the only pieces of Equipment available in the *Star Wars* universe. Samples of common [General Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/General_Equipment "General Equipment") found through the universe has been divided into several categories as shown below and are just as important for heroes' adventures:

Communications DevicesComputers and Storage DevicesDetection and Surveillance DevicesExplosivesLife SupportMedical GearSurvival GearToolsWeapon and Armor Accessories

[Communications Devices](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Communications_Devices "Communications Devices") in the *Star Wars Roleplaying Game* are assumed to use the same basic set of frequencies. The primary differences between them lie in range, size, and what kind of data (Audio, Video, or Holo) that they can carry. If two or more Communication Devices are within range of one another and share a Data Type, they can communicate.

[Computers and Storage Devices](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Computers_and_Storage_Devices "Computers and Storage Devices") include any electronic device that stores and processes data. Its [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence") score represents its processing capability, and is relevant when making [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") checks.

- If a computer's [Attitude](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") toward you is Friendly or Helpful, it grants an Equipment bonus equal to its [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence") bonus (if any) on any [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check you make using that computer.
- A computer's [Will Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Will_Defense "Will Defense") is equal to 15 + its [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence") modifier. When attempting to [Improve Access](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Improve_Access "Improve Access") to a computer, your [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check result must equal or exceed the computer's [Will Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Will_Defense "Will Defense").

**Storage Devices:** Some computers are very simple and used only for recording, storing, or viewing data. Some storage devices include a basic operating system and display that allows for manual viewing, entry, and editing of data, but these are more expensive.

Some [Detection and Surveillance Devices](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Detection_and_Surveillance_Devices "Detection and Surveillance Devices") augment a character's natural ability to perceive its environment (for example, [Electrobinoculars](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Electrobinoculars "Electrobinoculars")), providing a bonus or reducing penalties on [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") checks. Others use sensors to scan their surroundings beyond the normal visual and audible range.

Sometimes a mission calls for the use of more powerful [Explosives](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Explosives "Explosives") than either [Grenades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grenades "Grenades") or [Thermal Detonators](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Thermal_Detonators "Thermal Detonators"). Set Explosives aren't ranged weapons and can't be used like [Grenades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grenades "Grenades"). Placing an Explosive requires a [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check. When set and activated, the charge's Timer begins counting down. Standard Timers can be set with as much as an hour delay. Longer delays require specialized Timers.

With a successful [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, explosives ignore the [Damage Reduction](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Reduction "Damage Reduction") of objects to which they're attached. Particularly good [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check results can increase the damage even more. Multiple explosives rigged to explode at the same time deal extra damage: Every time you double the number of Explosives used, you add +2 dice of damage. For example, two blocks of [Detonite](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Detonite "Detonite") deal 7d6 points of damage, while four blocks deal 9d6 points of damage.

The *Star Wars Roleplaying Game* includes many hostile environments, ranging from deep oceans to poisonous atmospheres to the vacuum of space. [Life Support](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Life_Support "Life Support") devices enable creatures to function in such environments.

Medical equipment by itself does not restore lost [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points")- it can only help when used with the [Treat Injury](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Treat_Injury "Treat Injury") skill. Several common types of medical equipment are included in [Medical Gear](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Medical_Gear "Medical Gear").

Whether you're exploring the Dune Sea of [Tatooine](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tatooine "Tatooine") or scaling the volcanic mountains of [Mustafar](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mustafar "Mustafar"), you need the right equipment to survive. The most important considerations are food, water, shelter, and a way to signal for help. On many worlds, a lack of [Survival Gear](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Survival_Gear "Survival Gear") leads to a quick death.

Any objects designed to make a task easier- or take care of it entirely- are essentially [Tools](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tools "Tools"). Most technical jobs can be performed with a [Security Kit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Security_Kit "Security Kit") or [Tool Kit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tool_Kit "Tool Kit"), but some devices not included in those packages don't fit under any other Equipment category, and are presented here.

A number of off-the-shelf [Weapon and Armor Accessories](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_and_Armor_Accessories "Weapon and Armor Accessories") can enhance one's performance on the battlefield.

## Docking, Fuel, and Maintenance

*Reference Book: [Starships of the Galaxy](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships_of_the_Galaxy "Starships of the Galaxy")*

All [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") require refueling, restocking, routine maintenance, and a place to dock where they can get these services.

**Docking Fees:** Docking fees vary substantially from planet to planet, but they average around 20 credits per day in an average spaceport. Long-term storage is also available in some locations (200 credits per month). It generally requires about eight hours to get a ship out of storage and ready for flight.

**Refueling and Restocking:** All ships must have fuel to operate; a ship without fuel is adrift and immobile, effectively disabled. In general, most [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") refuel each time they put into port, and during refueling other essential ship consumables (such as water and oxygen) are restocked as well.

- *Refueling:* For a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") of Colossal or smaller size, refilling one day's worth of fuel (approximately 1 kg of fuel) costs about 50 credits. Larger [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") are substantially more expensive. Multiply the cost by 100 for every size category above Colossal. A [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") uses this much fuel after one day of flight in realspace or hyperspace, after one hour of flight in atmosphere or combat, or in a single jump to lightspeed.
- *Restocking:* Consumables cost about 10 credits x the number of living creatures on board x the days of operation.

**Routine Maintenance:** After twenty hyperspace jumps, a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") needs to undergo routine maintenance. Routine maintenance includes a thorough check of essential ship systems (including the backup [Hyperdrive](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hyperdrive "Hyperdrive") and [Escape Pods](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Escape_Pods "Escape Pods")), replacing coolant and other ship fluids, and a complete scrubbing of air filters and recycling systems. Routine Maintenance costs the same as about four days of fuel (200 credits for a ship of Colossal or lesser size). If a ship does not receive routine maintenance after twenty hyperspace jumps, roll twice when checking for [System Damage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Damage "System Damage").

## Money

The galactic economy turns on the wealth and products of billions of worlds. Throughout the history of known space, money has gone by many names, but the basic unit always came back to the "credit." As [The Galactic Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Republic "The Galactic Republic") waned and the rise of the Empire approached (circa Episodes I-III), the Republic credits (also called "dataries") no longer had much value beyond [The Core Worlds](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Core_Worlds?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Core Worlds (page does not exist)") and [The Inner Rim](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Inner_Rim?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Inner Rim (page does not exist)"). Instead, local currencies became popular. More often, people living and working in the distant regions wanted to trade in hard currency, not electronic credit chips. This changed when [The Galactic Empire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Empire "The Galactic Empire") came to power. By the time of the Rebellion era (circa Episode IV-VI), Imperial credits were accepted everywhere. Even [The Rebel Alliance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Rebel_Alliance "The Rebel Alliance") used this currency, as it was the standard throughout known space until the Empire finally fell.

[The New Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_New_Republic "The New Republic"), of course, minted its own credits after the victory at [Endor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Endor "Endor") and the establishment of the new galactic government. However, individual planets, regions, and sectors also adopted their own currencies, and the Imperial remnants issued their own scrip. Exchange rates fluctuated wildly throughout this period. The New Republic credit was established then years after the Battle of [Endor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Endor "Endor") and eventually emerged as the leading currency by the time of [The New Jedi Order Era](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_New_Jedi_Order_Era "The New Jedi Order Era").

The most common methods for keeping track of your credits are the [Credit Chip](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Credit_Chip "Credit Chip") or cred stick. These contain memory algorithms that can securely monitor the amount of credits available to the owner and accurately add to or subtract from that amount as transactions occur.

### Trade Goods

Some characters with access to a ship deal in commodities. Han Solo, for example, smuggled goods from place to place before hooking up with the Alliance. Other characters might be legitimate traders, buying and selling commodities to pay their way across the galaxy (and subsidize the adventures in which they wind up participating). Guilds and governments regulate trade. Charters and licenses are granted, cargo is regularly inspected, trade routes are established, and port fees are posted. In general, small freighters might come to the attention of local ports and the occasional inspection vessel, but independent traders are otherwise given a lot of freedom when they conduct their business.

The Gamemaster can get more specific, add or remove items depending on supply and demand in the campaign, and adjust prices to reflect such fluctuations in the market. ### Selling Items

Sometime you'll come into possession of an item that you don't want. We're not talking about commodities here. Commodities are valuable goods that can easily be exchanged almost like cash. We're talking about individual items from the lists and tables presented later on. In general, a merchant will buy used Equipment at half its listed price. If you have a [Blaster Pistol](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Blaster_Pistol "Blaster Pistol") and want to upgrade to a [Heavy Blaster Pistol](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Blaster_Pistol "Heavy Blaster Pistol"), you can sell the smaller blaster for half price.

## Restricted Items

Some objects require licenses to own or operate, or are restricted to qualifying organizations or individuals. In such cases, a character must pay a license fee to own the object legally. A license fee is a separate expense, purchased in addition to the object to which it applies. The four restriction ratings are as follows, as well "Rare" items:

- **Licensed:** The owner must obtain a license to own or operate the object legally. Generally, the license is not expensive, and obtaining it has few if any additional legal requirements.
- **Restricted:** Only specifically qualified individuals or organizations are technically allowed to own the object. However, the real obstacles to ownership are time and money; anyone with sufficient patience and cash can eventually acquire the necessary license.
- **Military:** The object is sold primarily to legitimate police and military organizations. A Military rating is essentially the same as Restricted, except that manufacturers and dealers are generally under tight government scrutiny and are therefore especially wary of selling to private individuals.
- **Illegal:** The object is Illegal in all but specific, highly regulated circumstances.
- **Rare:** A Rare piece of Equipment is generally available only on its planet of origin (for example, [Naboo](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Naboo "Naboo") for the [Atlatl](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Atlatl "Atlatl") and [Cesta](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Cesta "Cesta"), or [Kashyyyk](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Kashyyyk "Kashyyyk") for the [Bowcaster](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Bowcaster "Bowcaster")) or by acquiring it directly from the manufacturer. When available on the open market elsewhere, Rare items usually cost double the listed price.

While the galaxy is clenched in the iron fist of the Empire, a number of items that were once merely Rare or Restricted become Illegal contraband under the edicts of the New Order. As a general guideline, the availability tags for equipment in the *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")* are accurate representations of how available the item she should be during the reign of [The Galactic Empire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Empire "The Galactic Empire"), with a few exceptions. All weapons in the [Lightsabers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsabers "Lightsabers") weapon group are considered Illegal and Rare, though the Empire overlooks possession of such items for its chosen representatives. Additionally, items with a Military availability cannot be obtained legally through non-Imperial channels, even local planetary militias and system defense forces (most of which are absorbed by the Empire anyway). Military-grade items obtained on the Black Market are considered Illegal in the hands of private citizens, and those who carry them risk stiff penalties if they are discovered.

### Getting A License

To get a license, you must pay the fee required to file the application. The amount of the fee is a percentage of the object's normal cost, as listed in the below table. Once you've paid the fee, make a [Knowledge (Bureaucracy)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge_(Bureaucracy) "Knowledge (Bureaucracy)") check against the DC listed in the Skill DC column. You can't [Take 10](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Take_10 "Take 10") or [Take 20](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Take_20 "Take 20") on this check. On a success, your license is approved and will be available to you in a number of days as listed in the Time Required column. On a failure, you spend a number of days as listed in the Time Required column, but you are not granted the license and your application fee is lost. You may try again as often as you like if you have the time and credits to do so. Whether you succeed or fail on your [Knowledge (Bureaucracy)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge_(Bureaucracy) "Knowledge (Bureaucracy)") check, your request is recorded in public records. The more restricted the license, the more in-depth the background check required, and this leaves an increasingly detailed electronic trail for others to follow.

You can choose to secure a license through illicit means. If you want to bribe an official, make a [Persuasion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persuasion "Persuasion") check instead of a [Knowledge (Bureaucracy)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge_(Bureaucracy) "Knowledge (Bureaucracy)") check. If you want to fabricate a false identity or steal another person's identity, make a [Deception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Deception "Deception") check instead of a [Knowledge (Bureaucracy)](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge_(Bureaucracy) "Knowledge (Bureaucracy)") check. If either of these checks fails by 5 or more, the local authorities are alerted to your activities.

### The Black Market

*Reference Books: [Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook"), [Scum and Villainy](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scum_and_Villainy "Scum and Villainy")*

Almost anything is available on The Black Market. However, you must make a [Gather Information](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gather_Information "Gather Information") check to locate a Black Market merchant who has the object you seek. The DC of the check is listed in the Skill DC column of the above table, and the GM may apply a bonus or penalty to the check depending on the circumstances. (For example, finding a Black Market dealer on the smuggler's moon of [Nar Shaddaa](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa "Nar Shaddaa") is relatively easy and may warrant a +5 bonus on the check.) If you succeed on the [Gather Information](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gather_Information "Gather Information") check, you find a Black Market merchant who has access to the item you seek. If you fail, you can try again later. If you fail by 5 or more, someone notices you've been asking questions and comes to capture, interrogate, or silence you.

Once you find someone who can get the item for you, you'll have to pay two, three, four, or five times the item's normal price (as listed in the Black Market Cost column) and wait some time for the item to become available (as listed in the Time Required column).

The Black Market is the lifeblood of the fringe of the galaxy. It is through The Black Market that smugglers, shipjackers, pirates, and other criminals make their livings. The Black Market is the name given to the thousands of channels by which illegal goods are trafficked; this covers the fences that sell the items to buyers, the credit launderers that make sure dirty money looks clean, and the crime lords that organize and protect the members of their organization that engage in illegal commerce. The Black Market is the only way to obtain illicit items, and any character who wants to obtain something secretly must deal with The Black Market in some way.

The *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")* lists the basics of dealing with The Black Market, but in a [Fringe Campaign](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Campaign "Fringe Campaign") Gamemasters might want to make The Black Market a more significant feature. The following section outlines some more in-depth methods of involving The Black Market in a campaign. However, Gamemasters should be careful in making The Black Market more complicated than it needs to be; if the heroes need to buy items on The Black Market and you do not wish to consume a large portion of a gaming session dealing with the finding of contacts and haggling over items, just use the basic rules presented in the *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*.

#### Finding a Black Market Agent

Agents of The Black Market do not make themselves known easily, as they would be quickly swept up by the authorities. At its most basic, finding a Black Market agent requires a [Gather Information](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gather_Information "Gather Information") check, with a Skill DC based on the Restriction of the item you are trying to obtain. Hunting for a Black Market agent requires one to traverse back alleys and seedy cantinas, asking the right questions and trying to find an illicit dealer without getting reported to the authorities, or taking a blaster bolt to the back.

Even searching for The Black Market can be dangerous. A failed [Gather Information](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gather_Information "Gather Information") check shouldn't just result in a delay; it could also result in an encounter. If the heroes are snooping around and asking a lot of questions about The Black Market, local crime lords may get the wrong idea and send a few thugs to deter the heroes from inquiring further. On the other hand, if the heroes are careless, they might find themselves on the wrong side of the law, with law enforcement officials closing the net around them to arrest them on suspicion of dealing in illegal goods.

#### Selling on the Black Market

The Black Market can be used by the heroes not only to obtain illicit goods but also to sell them. If the heroes are smugglers, shipjackers, or pirates, they may need some way to pawn off their prizes, and The Black Market is an excellent place to do so. The heroes can find The Black Market in the same way that they would to buy from The Black Market, but instead of making purchases they can make sales, using the same Black Market Cost modifiers to determine how many credits the heroes make off their goods. The value of the goods is still halved as normal, before applying the Black Market modifiers (which should compensate for the reduction in value from selling the item).

#### Black Market Adventures

Gamemasters wishing to use The Black Market in an adventure have several options available to them. The most obvious method is to have either criminals working with The Black Market or law enforcement officials seeking to shut down The Black Market targeting the heroes and confronting them. Aside from combat encounters, The Black Market can be a good source for [Skill Challenges](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Challenges "Skill Challenges"); for example, a fence might be willing to sell an item on behalf of the heroes, but only if the heroes can make a [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check to disable an electronic lock on a [Spacer's Chest](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Spacer%27s_Chest "Spacer's Chest") the fence has in his possession but has been unable to open.

Additionally, The Black Market can be used as a lure to draw the heroes to a particular planet or locale; if the Gamemaster wants to set an adventure on [Nar Shaddaa](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa "Nar Shaddaa"), hinting that the heroes may have better luck in finding The Black Market safely on [Nar Shaddaa](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nar_Shaddaa "Nar Shaddaa") should be a big incentive to travel to the urban world.
