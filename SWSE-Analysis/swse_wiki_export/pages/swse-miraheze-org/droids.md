# Droids

> Source: [https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droids) · Retrieved 2026-10-09T09:36:25Z.

*Reference Books: [Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook"), [Threats of the Galaxy](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Threats_of_the_Galaxy "Threats of the Galaxy"), [Scavenger's Guide to Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scavenger%27s_Guide_to_Droids "Scavenger's Guide to Droids")*

A Droid is a kind of intelligent robot, a mechanical automaton electronically programmed to act, think, and behave in a certain way. Droids facilitate various tasks that organic beings find tedious, difficult, or hazardous. They are typically fashioned in the likeness of their creators or in a utilitarian design that stresses function. Their usefulness makes them a common sight on nearly every inhabited world in the galaxy. They provide assistance, advice, and sometimes even friendship to trillions of sentient beings on a daily basis.

Droids are usually property, bought and sold like any other piece of equipment. Although some Droid owners come to think of their Droids as friends, the fact remains that Droids are programmed to serve whoever is designated as their master. To the vast majority of Droids, the concept of Droid independence is unthinkable. Without someone to command them, what would they do?

Even so, ownership and control are two very different things. Droids must do what they are commanded to do to the best of their ability, but their programming dictates how they accomplish their orders. Ordered to find a replacement part for an [X-Wing](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/X-Wing "X-Wing"), a Droid might commence a systematic search of the repair bay, then the docking facility, then the neighborhood, then the countryside, and so on- all while thinking its actions are perfectly logical. Droids often need very specific instructions to do what their masters command, in a manner meeting their master's expectations.

Occasionally, events conspire to lead a Droid to independence. These so-called "self-owned" Droids are few, but not as rare as many believe. One might hear stories of Droids that escape their masters after years of abuse, or of an entire line of Droids that manifest violent personalities as the result of a programming glitch. However, [Independent Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Independent_Droids "Independent Droids") are rarely haywire, rampaging killers. [Independent Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Independent_Droids "Independent Droids") often enter into partnerships organic beings, particularly on worlds where a lone Droid without a master draws a lot of unwanted attention. [Independent Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Independent_Droids "Independent Droids") sometimes even seek each other out, hoping to find strength and security in numbers. Some [Independent Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Independent_Droids "Independent Droids") are thrust into the unlikely role of hero, helping to defend the galaxy from the depredations of evil as personified by the likes of the Empire and the [Yuuzhan Vong](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Yuuzhan_Vong "Yuuzhan Vong").

## Droid Traits

As inorganic mechanical beings, all Droids have certain attributes that affect how they interact with their environment and other beings.

- **Ability Modifiers:** Droids are nonliving entities, so they do not have [Constitution](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution") scores. Droids can increase any two of their five remaining [Ability Scores](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Scores "Ability Scores") by +1 each at 4th level and every four levels thereafter, just like any other character. These increases represent improved heuristics and algorithms that the Droid has developed from experience as well as upgrades to its components undertaken as part of routine maintenance.
  - Droid Ability modifiers are determined by their Degree and Size. A Droid can never have an [Ability Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Score "Ability Score") less than 1, regardless of modifications.
- **Automatic Languages:** All Droids can speak, read, and process [Binary](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Binary "Binary"), as well as understand one language chosen by the designer (usually Basic).
- **Behavioral Inhibitors:** Droids (except [4th-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/4th-Degree_Droids "4th-Degree Droids")) cannot intentionally harm a sentient living being or knowingly allow a sentient living being to be harmed. Furthermore, all Droids must follow orders given to them by their rightful owners, as long as those orders don't require the Droid to harm a sentient living being.
  - Droids with [Heuristic Processors](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processors "Heuristic Processors") can sometimes violate these restrictions by creatively interpreting their behavioral inhibitors.
  - The only consistent way to avoid these limitations is to reprogram the Droid with programming from a [4th-Degree Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/4th-Degree_Droid "4th-Degree Droid"), which requires a DC 35 [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check if the Droid was not originally designed to be [4th-Degree](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/4th-Degree "4th-Degree").
- **Ion Damage Vulnerability:** As electronic constructs, Droids are vulnerable to damage from Ion weapons. Generally, [Ion Damage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ion_Damage "Ion Damage") has the same effects on Droids that [Stun Damage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stun_Damage "Stun Damage") has on living beings.
- **Maintenance:** Droids do not sleep, eat, or breathe. However, they do need to enter shutdown mode and recharge for 1 hour after 100 hours of operation. If a Droid fails to do so, it must make an [Endurance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Endurance "Endurance") check each hour (DC 10, +1 per additional hour after the first) or move -1 [Persistent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent "Persistent") step along the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track").
  - This [Persistent Condition](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Condition "Persistent Condition") can only be removed by the Droid recharging for 1 hour.
- **Memory:** A Droid's [Trained Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skills "Trained Skills"), [Feats](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feats "Feats"), and [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") can be reassigned with the [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") skill (see [Reprogramming](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reprogramming "Reprogramming")). A Droid hero can use its own [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") skill to perform this [Reprogramming](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reprogramming "Reprogramming"), but it takes a -5 penalty on its skill check.
  - If a Droid is ever subjugated to a complete [Memory Wipe](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Memory_Wipe "Memory Wipe"), it becomes a basic model of its type, losing any levels and abilities gained.
- **Nonliving:** A Droid is immune to [Poison](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Poison "Poison"), [Disease](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Disease "Disease"), [Radiation](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Radiation "Radiation"), noncorrosive [Atmosphere](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Atmosphere "Atmosphere") Hazards, [Vacuum](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vacuum "Vacuum"), Mind-Affecting effects, [Stunning](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stunning "Stunning") effects, and any other effect that only works on living targets. Droids have no connection to [The Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Force "The Force") and can't gain the [Force Sensitivity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Sensitivity "Force Sensitivity") feat, or learn [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers").
  - Droids do not have a [Constitution](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution") score, so they don't get bonus [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") for having a high [Constitution](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution"), and they apply their [Strength](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Strength "Strength") modifier to their [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense").
  - Unlike living beings, Droids don't "die," but they can be [Disabled](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Disabled "Disabled") or [Destroyed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destroyed "Destroyed"). Droids do not gain an ability bonus to [Constitution](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution")-based skill checks and may not take [Feats](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feats "Feats") or [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") with a [Constitution](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution") prerequisite.
  - If a Droid is reduced to 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points"), it is [Disabled](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Disabled "Disabled") and cannot be reactivated until it is [Repaired](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Repaired "Repaired") so that it has at least 1 [Hit Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Point "Hit Point"). If the attack that reduced the Droid to 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") also exceeds the Droid's [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"), the Droid is [Destroyed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destroyed "Destroyed") instead. A Destroyed Droid cannot be repaired or salvaged.
- **Repair:** Droids can regain lost [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") only through the use of the [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") skill (see [Repair](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Repair "Repair")). A Droid can use this application to repair itself, but it takes a -5 penalty on its skill check.
- **Shut Down:** A Droid that is Shut Down can take no actions and is effectively [Unconscious](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Unconscious "Unconscious"). Shutting down a willing Droid is a [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action"). Shutting down an unwilling Droid is more difficult, requiring you to [Grab](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grab "Grab") the Droid, and then make a [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check (DC = Droid's [Will Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Will_Defense "Will Defense")) as a [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action") while it's [Grabbed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grabbed "Grabbed").
  - You cannot shut down an unwilling Droid with [Locked Access](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Locked_Access "Locked Access") unless it is [Disabled](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Disabled "Disabled") or otherwise [Helpless](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Helpless "Helpless").
- **Skills:** Droids normally cannot use any [Skill](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill "Skill") Untrained except for [Acrobatics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Acrobatics "Acrobatics"), [Climb](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Climb "Climb"), [Jump](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jump "Jump"), and [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception"). A Droid with a [Heuristic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processor "Heuristic Processor") ignores this limitation.
- **Systems:** Droids can have many of their characteristics changed by installing or replacing existing Droid Systems.

## Droid Heroes

If you want to play a Droid as a hero, you can either play an existing Droid from this section or create your own unique Droid hero. Droid heroes are assumed either to be "self-owned" or to be owned by another player character (with the approval of both the Gamemaster and the player in question). Even if owned by another member of the party, the Gamemaster should ensure that the owner does not abuse their authority by giving unreasonable or unwanted orders to the other player's Droid character.

A Droid player character should be treated the same as any other player character, and in most cases it is best to give suggestions or advice instead of actual orders. (In fact, most Droid heroes have a [Heuristic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processor "Heuristic Processor") so that they can creatively interpret orders and thus justify acting as they wish.) When you decide to play a Droid hero, you'll have to pick out a few details about what type of Droid your character will be. This is the equivalent of picking a [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") for a non-Droid character.

### Playing a Custom Droid

You can play a Droid of your own design. The only choices you have to make are choosing your Degree, size, accessories, and assigning your [Ability Scores](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Scores "Ability Scores"). Once you have taken note of these details on your [Character Sheet](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Character_Sheet "Character Sheet"), continue with [Character Creation](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Character_Creation "Character Creation") normally.

Droid characters determine their [Ability Scores](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Scores "Ability Scores") the same way non-Droid characters do. However, Droids do not have [Constitution](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution "Constitution") scores because they are not actually living beings; they only need scores for their five remaining [Abilities](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Abilities "Abilities"). You can determine your [Ability Scores](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Scores "Ability Scores") in one of the following ways:

- **Rolling:** Roll 4d6 five times, discarding the lowest die each time. Add up the remaining three dice and assign the result to any one of your five [Abilities](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Abilities "Abilities").
- **Planned Generation:** All of your Ability Scores start at 8, and you have 21 points to spend to increase them. (Refer to [Planned Generation](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Planned_Generation "Planned Generation").)
- **Standard Score Package:** The Standard Score Package for Droid heroes is 15, 14, 13, 12, and 10. Assign these five scores to your five [Abilities](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Abilities "Abilities") as you see fit.

Droids are classified by Degree, reflecting the types of tasks they typically perform. Generally, [1st-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/1st-Degree_Droids "1st-Degree Droids") are medical and analytical Droids, [2nd-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/2nd-Degree_Droids "2nd-Degree Droids") are mechanical and technical Droids, [3rd-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/3rd-Degree_Droids "3rd-Degree Droids") are protocol and domestic Droids, [4th-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/4th-Degree_Droids "4th-Degree Droids") are security and battle Droids, and [5th-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/5th-Degree_Droids "5th-Degree Droids") are labor and utility Droids. A Droid's function does not have to match its Degree, but this is uncommon.

Pick a Degree (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, or 5th) for your Droid. This determines your [Ability Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Score "Ability Score") modifiers, as shown in the table below. For example, a 3rd-Degree Protocol Droid increases its [Wisdom](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wisdom "Wisdom") and [Charisma](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charisma "Charisma") scores by 2 and reduces its [Strength](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Strength "Strength") score by 2.

You can choose to play a Medium or Small Droid. Droids of other sizes exist, but they are controlled by the GM. A Droid's size determines its [Ability](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability "Ability") modifiers, its size bonus to [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense"), its size bonus on [Stealth](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stealth "Stealth") checks, adjustments to [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") and [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"), its [Carrying Capacity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Carrying_Capacity "Carrying Capacity"), and its Cost Factor as shown in the table below.

**Medium Droids:** Player character Droids of medium size have no special modifiers because of their size. They have a [Walking](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Walking "Walking") Locomotion System and a base speed of 6 squares.

**Small Droids:** Player Droids of small size apply the following [Ability Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Score "Ability Score") modifiers: +2 [Dexterity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dexterity "Dexterity"), -2 [Strength](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Strength "Strength"). They have a [Tracked](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tracked "Tracked") Locomotion System and a base speed of 4 squares. Small Droids gain a +1 size bonus to their [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense") and a +5 size bonus on [Stealth](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stealth "Stealth") checks. However, their lifting and [Carrying Capacity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Carrying_Capacity "Carrying Capacity") limits are three-quarters of those of Medium characters.

**Cost Factor:** A Droid's Cost Factor is used to calculate the cost of the Droid and some Droid Systems. Particularly large Droids can be very expensive, but particularly small Droids are not any cheaper due to the expenses associated with miniaturization. Droids that are Small size or smaller treat their Cost Factor as being equal to 2 divided by their Cost Factor for the purposes of determining Droid Accessory weight.

**Class and Level:** You are 1st-level in one [Heroic Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heroic_Class "Heroic Class") ([Noble](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Noble "Noble"), [Scoundrel](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scoundrel "Scoundrel"), [Scout](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scout "Scout"), or [Soldier](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Soldier "Soldier")) of your choice. ### Playing a Standard Droid Model

If you wish to forego creating your own Custom Droid, you can always play one of the stock Droid Models created in bulk. If you use this option, you do not roll for [Ability Scores](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Scores "Ability Scores"); instead, you automatically have the [Ability Scores](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Scores "Ability Scores") listed for the selected model of Droid. The Droid you select must meet the following criteria, and you may modify the stock model as noted below.

- **Size:** You can only be a Small or Medium-sized Droid.
- **Processor:** You cannot have a [Remote Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Remote_Processor "Remote Processor"), so you must install a [Basic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Basic_Processor "Basic Processor") or [Heuristic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processor "Heuristic Processor") if the selected model of Droid does not normally include one.
- **Class and Level:** The Droid you select can have no more than three levels in the [Nonheroic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nonheroic "Nonheroic") class or one level in any [Heroic Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heroic_Class "Heroic Class"). If the Droid you select has only one or two levels in the [Nonheroic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nonheroic "Nonheroic") class, you may choose to add one level in a [Heroic Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heroic_Class "Heroic Class") of your choice; this adds 1,500 credits to your Droid's final cost.
  - You begin play with the minimum number of [Experience Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Experience_Points "Experience Points") necessary for your Character Level. For example, a 2nd-level [Nonheroic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nonheroic "Nonheroic")/1st-level [Scoundrel](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scoundrel "Scoundrel") would begin play with 3,000 XP.
- **Final Cost:** The final cost of your Droid, including any necessary adjustments for a Processor or adding a level in a [Heroic Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heroic_Class "Heroic Class"), cannot exceed 5,000 credits. You may add Droid Accessories or replace Droid Systems as you see fit as long as your final cost remains within this limit. You do not get to keep any unspent credits, but you still gain the appropriate starting credits according to your [Heroic Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heroic_Class "Heroic Class").
- **Droid Traits:** All Droid characters have certain traits in common as discussed in Droid Traits, above.

### Playing a Stock Droid Chassis

Players can select a stock Droid Chassis when creating a Droid Hero. This streamlined method of Droid creation treats each Droid Chassis as a [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species"). Each Droid Chassis represents the basis for creating a type of Droid rather than a specific model, so a player can quickly generate a new model of Droid, or emulate an existing model while building a character entirely with Heroic Levels. Stock Droid Chassis can also be used to create specific models of Droids, since the chassis share features of the specific Droid Models of that class of Droid.

However, the customization required to make the Droid Chassis match up with a specific Droid Model in question might make other methods more appealing. In some cases, an existing model might not be exactly replicated, but the differences can be attributed to variances in production or aftermarket customizations to the standard model. The Size and Speed of a Droid Chassis cannot be altered without Gamemaster approval. To create a Droid Hero using a stock Droid Chassis, follow these steps:

1. **Select the Chassis.** Apply [Ability](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability "Ability") modifiers, Size, Speed, Chassis Traits, [Languages](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Languages "Languages"), and other modifiers as you would when applying a [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") to a character.
2. **Review the Chassis' Bonus Equipment.** If you are creating a new or generic Droid without a specific model in mind, leave the Droid Systems as they are and proceed to the next step. If you are creating a Droid of a specific model, compare this list to systems listed in the desired Droid's statistics block. If they are close enough for your purposes, proceed to the next step. An unwanted system can be swapped for another system within 10% of the unwanted system's cost without spending any credits. However, if upgraded or additional systems are required, they must be purchased with the Droid's starting credits (see below).
3. **Starting Credits:** Your Droid's [Heroic Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heroic_Class "Heroic Class") determines how many starting credits you have. You can spend your starting credits on Droid Systems as well as on normal [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"). ## Droid Systems

Droids are mechanical beings with interchangeable parts. As such, the functionality of Droids range as widely as the organic [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") of *Star Wars*. Droids can be differentiated between one another based on their Droid Model, as well as their own Droid Systems. While the Model represents the Droid's function, the modified Droid Systems a Droid installs can show a Droid's personality and specializations.

**Cost Factor:** A Droid's Cost Factor is used to calculate the cost of some Droid Systems. Droids that are Small size or smaller treat their Cost Factor as being equal to 2 divided by their Cost Factor for the purposes of determining Droid Accessory weight. Cost Factors are determined by a Droid's Size:

A Droids' Systems can be upgraded, replaced, and modified many times throughout a Droid's operational lifetime. A Droid System falls into one of four categories:

Add 500 x the Droid's Cost Factor for the second Locomotion System, 1,000 x the Droid's Cost Factor for the third, 2,000 x the Droid's Cost Factor for the fourth, 5,000 x the Droid's Cost Factor for the fifth. A Droid can't perform any functions without its Processor (also known as a Droid Brain), which contains all the basic information the Droid needs to move its appendages, travel from place to place, behave in a certain way, and so on. A Droid's [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence") score reflects the quality of its Processor. Low-intelligence Droids tend to specialize in single tasks that require no deductive capability. High-intelligence Droids are considerably more versatile- and more expensive.

### Behavioral Inhibitors

Even without a restraining bolt or periodic [Memory Wipes](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Memory_Wipes "Memory Wipes"), most Droids operate according to a rigid set of guidelines. A Droid's core programming- the part of its memory that can't be wiped- provides it with strict instructions on how to react to common circumstances, most of which revolve around obedience, safety, ethics, and morality. These instructions are the Droid's Behavioral Inhibitors.

The most common restriction coded into a Droid's memory is the notion that it can't harm a sentient living being or, through inaction, allow a sentient living being to come to harm. ([4th-Degree Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/4th-Degree_Droids "4th-Degree Droids") do not have this restriction.) Droids are under similar restrictions not to allow themselves to be harmed unless specifically ordered to do so. Of course, Droids are also hardwired to obey the commands of their designated masters. When a master's orders conflict with the Droid's Behavioral Inhibitors, the Droid is required to inform its master immediately.

- **Maximum Dexterity Bonus:** Built-in Droid armor has a maximum [Dexterity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dexterity "Dexterity") bonus, just like normal [Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armor "Armor").
- **Availability:** Some armors have limited availability.
  - *Rare:* This armor is rarely for sale on the open market. The price of the armor on the black market is usually double the listed cost.
  - *Licensed, Restricted, Military, or Illegal:* Ownership of the armor is limited or strictly regulated, as described in [Restricted Items](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Restricted_Items "Restricted Items").

### Modifying Droids

A Droid can be modified to carry additional [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment") simply by attaching the new [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment") to the Droid's chassis and connecting the new component to the Droid's Processor. Adding Equipment increases the Droid's weight. Droids suffer from [Encumbrance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Encumbrance "Encumbrance") penalties the same way organics characters do: they slow down. Adding, removing, or replacing the systems on a Droid requires a [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, the DC set by the type of Droid System.

- **Locomotion:** DC 25 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, representing 1 day of work.
- **Processor:** DC 20 [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check and DC 20 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, representing 1 day of work.
- **Appendage:** DC 20 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, representing 1 hour of work.
- **Droid Accessory:** DC 15 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, representing 1 hour of work.
- **Exchange [Tool Mount](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tool_Mount "Tool Mount") or [Instrument](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Instrument "Instrument"):** DC 15 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check, representing 10 minutes of work. You take a -5 penalty on your [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check when installing a weapon on a 1st, 2nd, 3rd, or 5th Degree Droid.
- **Self-Modification:** A Droid may install or replace a Locomotion System, Appendage, or Droid Accessory on itself, but it takes a -5 penalty on its [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") checks. A Droid cannot replace or install a Processor on itself.

#### Adjusting Droid Modifications

*Reference Book: [Scavenger's Guide to Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scavenger%27s_Guide_to_Droids "Scavenger's Guide to Droids")*

One of the biggest challenges a Gamemaster faces when dealing with Droids in the party is what to do when the players start modifying the Droids. Through the Droid Modification rules, players might attempt modifications that seriously alter the nature of the game in a way that you have not anticipated. The best thing to do when a player attempts to introduce a modification that isn't appropriate for the game is to stop it before it happens. Be up-front in explaining that you don't think the modification is appropriate for the game, and try to find another solution that works for both you and the player. Remember, just because a piece of equipment or modification appears in a book doesn't mean that your players should automatically be able to perform that modification or install that equipment. There are a number of factors- social, economic, and legal- that can preclude the production or modification of a particular Droid.

With all that said, sometimes Droids in your game may be modified to a point where they are disrupting the game. Below is some advice on handling problematic Droids and Droid Modifications. Note that most of this advice centers on Droids introduced as Gamemaster characters and possessions, not on Droids as heroes.

Unbalancing ModificationRedundant ModificationTaking Away Toys

One of the most obvious problems you will come across as a Gamemaster is a Droid that has been modified in such a way as to be unbalancing in encounters. Either the Droid is making encounters too easy, or the Droid is overshadowing the contributions of the other heroes, or the Droid just makes one player too much the focus of the combat round. Maybe the player installed shields on the Droid that make it nigh invincible. Whatever the actual modification, your best bet is to consider complications that can arise as a result of owning this Droid.

Suppose a Droid is a combat monster, blowing through your encounters. Let the player have his or her fun for a few encounters. Then create complications. Maybe the Droid isn't as precise as it should be, and it ends up causing collateral damage. Maybe the Droid ends up firing on innocent bystanders, or destroys a passing [Airspeeder](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Airspeeder "Airspeeder") just to take out some [Stormtroopers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stormtroopers "Stormtroopers"). The Droid then becomes a liability, and the heroes need to decide whether or not it's worth keeping the Droid around. Likewise, a savvy slicer might reprogram the Droid, turning it against the heroes. Even after the heroes subdue it, there will always be some lingering doubt as to whether or not all of the traitorous programming was eradicated. When a Droid is too powerful, introduce drawbacks that require the heroes to carefully consider the Droid's value.

Another troubling aspect of Droids and Droid Modifications is that sometimes a modified Droid can make other Droids, and even other heroes, feel obsolete. Maybe the Droid has every [Knowledge](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge") Skill [Trained](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained "Trained"), making the [Noble](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Noble "Noble") who invested [Trained Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skills "Trained Skills") in [Knowledge](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge") Skills feel like they wasted resources. Maybe the [Ace Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ace_Pilot "Ace Pilot") suddenly finds themselves sitting in the copilot's chair while the Droid takes the helm. By raw statistics, there are many ways in which a Droid can be modified to do things that the heroes do, and better.

When this happens, consider altering the way the Droid performs its task so that, while it gets the job done, maybe it doesn't do so by the easiest means. When the Droid is turned loose to make a [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check to repair the ship, it starts installing "improvements" that manifest elsewhere. When the Protocol Droid programmed with every [Knowledge](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge") Skill is brought along to answer every question, perhaps it answers in roundabout ways that reveal information that the players want to keep secret. Droids, like all beings, are flawed, and whenever a Droid has been modified to obviate other characters or Droids, those flaws should be brought to the forefront. As with the situation above, it's more interesting to create a choice for your players (keep the Droid or get rid of it) than to simply take the Droid away.

There may be times when you have no choice but to take the Droid away from the players. This is a last resort, but it's always a possibility. Whether you have the Droid blasted, smashed, disassembled, stolen, or simply have its memory wiped, your players are going to feel as though you're taking away their toys. When you have no other option but to take the droid away from the heroes, consider the following guidelines:

- **Make the Droid's removal part of the plot:** Don't just have a [Stormtrooper](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stormtrooper "Stormtrooper") shoot the Droid; have a Droid thief steal it in order to learn more about the heroes. Have the Droid's memory wiped by a malicious slicer hired by the heroes' enemies, or have its memory copied first and held as blackmail by an unscrupulous crime lord. Have the Droid destroyed as a part of a failed assassination attempt. Whatever form your removal of the Droid takes, set it up to create a new adventure and story possibilities.
- **Offer a replacement for the Droid:** When you take something away from the heroes, don't forget to give something back. The replacement for the Droid doesn't always have to be another Droid, either; maybe the heroes meet a [Squib](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Squib "Squib") mechanic with many of the same skills as the stolen Droid, or perhaps the heroes' [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") gets upgraded with a [Droid Brain](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid_Brain "Droid Brain") thanks to their benefactor. Whatever you give as a replacement should obviously be free of the same problems that caused you to take the Droid away in the first place, but figure out what good things the Droid brought to the group and find a suitable replacement for that.
- **Give the heroes a chance to recover the Droid:** If you simply take the Droid away, it will likely be very obvious to your players that you removed it because you didn't like what it was doing to the game. If a Droid is stolen, or its memories taken, or its parts scavenged by [Jawas](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jawas "Jawas"), sometimes you'll have the option of letting your players try and get those things back. Of course, when the Droid is restored it may not be exactly the same as before, but this is an opportunity to remove the modifications that created the problem, and it helps make the Droid into an evolving character.

## [Droid Manufacturers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid_Manufacturers "Droid Manufacturers")

*Reference Book: [Scavenger's Guide to Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scavenger%27s_Guide_to_Droids "Scavenger's Guide to Droids")*

Millions of manufacturing companies produce Droids. Most are small companies that cater to local regions, manufacture specialized units, or focus on highly customized models. The major interstellar manufacturers dominate specific markets, although those markets shift as the demands of the galactic government and mega corporations change.

Throughout [The Clone Wars](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Clone_Wars "The Clone Wars") and the Galactic Civil War, [Cybot Galactica](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Cybot_Galactica "Cybot Galactica") and [Industrial Automaton](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Industrial_Automaton "Industrial Automaton") are the dominant manufacturers, with [Arakyd Industries](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Arakyd_Industries "Arakyd Industries"), [MerenData](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/MerenData "MerenData"), [Serv-O-Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Serv-O-Droid "Serv-O-Droid"), and [Veril Line Systems](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Veril_Line_Systems "Veril Line Systems") as major players in their respective markets. During [The Clone Wars](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Clone_Wars "The Clone Wars"), most Separatist Battle Droids are manufactured by [Baktoid Combat Automata](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Baktoid_Combat_Automata "Baktoid Combat Automata") forces, with other units coming from [The Techno Union](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Techno_Union "The Techno Union"), [Colicoid Creation Nest](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Colicoid_Creation_Nest "Colicoid Creation Nest"), and [The Commerce Guild](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Commerce_Guild "The Commerce Guild"). However, Droid manufacturing has existed for thousands of years. In the time of [The Old Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Old_Republic "The Old Republic"), 4,000 years before [The Rebellion Era](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Rebellion_Era "The Rebellion Era"), [The Czerka Corporation](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Czerka_Corporation "The Czerka Corporation"), [Duwani Mechanical Products](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Duwani_Mechanical_Products "Duwani Mechanical Products"), and [Aratech Repulsor Company](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Aratech_Repulsor_Company?action=edit&redlink=1 "Aratech Repulsor Company (page does not exist)") dominated the Droid markets of their day; other well-known manufacturers, such as [MerenData](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/MerenData "MerenData") and [Serv-O-Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Serv-O-Droid "Serv-O-Droid"), were also becoming competitive.

Although manufacturing plants can be found on both major and lesser worlds, a significant percentage of Droids come from either [Mechis III](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechis_III "Mechis III") or [Telti](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Telti "Telti"). These two worlds specialize in Droid production to such an extent that their surfaces are essentially covered by automated precision-manufacturing plants. The major manufacturers use these fabrication facilities extensively. Most of the Droids built in these facilities are shipped by bulk freighter, but light freighter jockeys make a decent living running small orders to isolated regions of the galaxy.

## Adjudicating Droids

*Reference Book: [Scavenger's Guide to Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scavenger%27s_Guide_to_Droids "Scavenger's Guide to Droids")*

Adjudicating Droids can present unique challenges for the Gamemaster. Although they are essentially living machines, Droids cannot be treated as simply another [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") or character type. Below are common Droid issues and information on adjudicating them.

Damaged and Destroyed DroidsImprovised Droid TasksThe Droid that Gained LevelsDroid Data Ports

Damaged and destroyed Droids are treated similarly to organic heroes when it comes to damage and death. However, Droids are also machines and can be reassembled or have their data accessed by other means, so they have additional options not available to organic characters. New approaches to damage and destruction are presented below. Information on Droid damage and repair from the *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")* is also included here for convenience.

- **Conditions:** A Droid is treated the same as an organic character when it comes to the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track") and [Persistent Conditions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Conditions "Persistent Conditions"). Unlike organic characters, however, a damaged Droid might gain a Droid Quirk.
- **Destroyed:** If a Droid is reduced to 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") by an attack that deals damage equal to or greater than its [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"), the Droid is Destroyed. However, it can spend a [Force Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Point "Force Point") to become Disabled instead of Destroyed.
- **Disabled:** When a Droid is Disabled (the equivalent of being [Unconscious](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Unconscious "Unconscious")), it moves -5 steps on the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"), falls [Prone](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Prone "Prone"), and is unable to take any [Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Actions "Actions"). It remains inert and inoperative until repaired. A Droid that is repaired immediately reactivates and can get up to fight again, but it starts [Prone](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Prone "Prone").
- **Repair Droid (Requires [Tool Kit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tool_Kit "Tool Kit")):** A character with [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") as a [Trained Skill](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skill "Trained Skill") can spend 1 hour of work and make a DC 20 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check to repair a Damaged or Disabled Droid, restoring [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") equal to the Droid's Character Level and removing any [Persistent Conditions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Conditions "Persistent Conditions") currently affecting the Droid. A Droid can attempt to repair itself, but it takes a -5 penalty on its [Skill Check](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Check "Skill Check").

## New Optional Rules

The following new optional rules can be included, in whole or in part, in your game to give Droid Heroes some of the versatility seen in the *Star Wars* films. As with all optional rules, inclusion of the following rules in the game is subject to Gamemaster approval.

- **Sacrificial Appendage:** When a Droid would normally be reduced to 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") or moved to the bottom of the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"), the Droid can choose instead to have one of its limbs severed. If the Droid chooses this option, it is instead moved to the -4 step on the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"), or, in the case of being reduced to 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points"), it retains 1 [Hit Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Point "Hit Point"). The sacrificed [Appendage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Appendage "Appendage") should be a limb rather than a tool that the Droid can easily do without. The [Appendage](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Appendage "Appendage") can be successfully reattached with a repair check (see [Repair Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Repair_Droid "Repair Droid")). The Droid receives [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") from the repair, as usual.
- **Destroyed Droids:** Taking into account the method of destruction, the Gamemaster determines what parts of a Droid remain after it is destroyed. Blaster fire or an explosion might scatter small and large pieces around the area. A Droid destroyed by a [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber") might have slices or holes burned through its chassis, or large sections of the Droid's appendages might be completely severed. The Gamemaster should also determine which of the Droid's systems, accessories, appendages, or tools are damaged or destroyed.
- **Reactivating a Destroyed Droid:** At the Gamemaster's discretion, a character can attempt to salvage and reactivate a destroyed Droid. Reactivating a Droid requires that its [Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Processor "Processor"), power source, and communication interface must be intact. The battery and communication interface can be replaced or replicated if needed. A destroyed Droid cannot reactivate itself. The character attempting the reactivation must make a DC 20 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check and work on the Droid for 5 minutes.
  - A character can attempt to reactivate a Droid as a [Full-Round Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full-Round_Action "Full-Round Action"), increasing the DC to 30. If the check is successful, the Droid moves + 1 step on the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"). However, the Droid is still considered to have 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") and cannot move, attack, or take any action other than [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") for [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") based on [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence"), [Wisdom](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wisdom "Wisdom"), or [Charisma](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charisma "Charisma").
- **Salvaging a Destroyed Droid:** Salvaging a destroyed Droid requires a [Tool Kit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tool_Kit "Tool Kit"), time, spare parts, and additional [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") checks (see [Repair Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Repair_Droid "Repair Droid")). A reactivated Droid can assist in its own repairs by using the [Aid Another](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Aid_Another "Aid Another") Action, although it cannot attempt to repair itself directly. The time required to salvage a Droid can be affected by the level of destruction sustained by the Droid. Repairs might take several hours, several days, or even several weeks, as determined by the Gamemaster. Destroyed Droids usually require spare parts costing 50% or more of the Droid's original cost.

Heroes might try to take maximum advantage of the unique resources a droid offers. A Droid's built-in equipment, traits, and unique social status in the *Star Wars* universe cause Droids and Droid Heroes to be called upon to carry out tasks well beyond their original programming.

When considering an unusual request for or by a Droid character, consider the following:

- **Does the Droid have a [Heuristic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processor "Heuristic Processor")?** If not, then the request must be governed by the standards of the [Basic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Basic_Processor "Basic Processor") or [Remote Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Remote_Processor "Remote Processor"), so the task must be laid out in specific terms. Unless the request is defined in ways the Droid understands, the Droid cannot act beyond its programmed [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills"), [Feats](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feats "Feats"), special abilities, and tasks. Even if the tasks are clearly delineated, the Droid might perform them poorly or inefficiently or become confused if the task does not proceed as planned.
  - A Droid equipped with a [Heuristic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processor "Heuristic Processor") is better equipped to handle unusual situations, so it can act on fewer, less specific instructions. Additionally, focusing on the advantages of the [Heuristic Processor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heuristic_Processor "Heuristic Processor") emphasizes the choice of processor in Droid creation, rewarding those players who select it.
- **Is the request already covered by an existing [Talent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talent "Talent"), [Feat](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feat "Feat"), or Droid special quality?** Don't shortchange players who select certain Heroic Class, Droid, or Droid Prestige Class [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") and [Feats](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feats "Feats") by allowing other Droids to replicate those abilities too often. If it seems reasonable that a given Droid might be capable of an action similar to an existing ability, allow the action to proceed with a lesser chance of success and/or a lesser result than could be gained with the existing ability.
- **Is the request likely to be abused by the player in future sessions, or likely to become exploited regularly?** If not, allow the idea to be used at least in this instance. However, if the idea could become an unwelcome addition to future sessions, the Gamemaster can avoid denying its use outright by allowing the character to spend a [Force Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Point "Force Point") or a [Destiny Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Point "Destiny Point") (if appropriate) for the action. Another option is for the Droid to suffer a [Persistent Condition](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Condition "Persistent Condition") and move -1 or more steps on the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track") at the end of the task.
- **Should the task be covered by new [Talent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talent "Talent"), [Feat](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feat "Feat"), or special quality?** Some tasks generated by players are more appropriately covered by creating a new Droid-degree [Talent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talent "Talent"), [Feat](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feat "Feat"), or special quality. If the player creates a wholly customized or new [Droid Model](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid_Model "Droid Model"), a special quality can be added at the Gamemaster's discretion.
  - If the Droid is an existing stock model, create a [Talent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talent "Talent") or a [Feat](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feat "Feat") instead. If the ability seems like something a Droid ought to be able to learn, create a [Feat](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feat "Feat"). If the ability seems focused on a trademark task of a particular model or degree of Droid, create a [Talent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talent "Talent") specific to that degree of Droid.
- **Adjudicating simple and complex tasks.** If the Gamemaster decides to proceed with the request, he or she should decide what actions, attacks, and [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") are required for success. Simple tasks are typically covered by one [Skill Check](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Check "Skill Check"), but unique circumstances might require more time and/or multiple checks. Complex tasks should take multiple rounds with multiple checks.
- **Plan for failure.** A Droid that performs a task outside its realm of expertise can make for potentially amusing or unexpected situations if it fails. The Droid might become confused, carry out the next operation without completing the first task, return unexpectedly, call on the owner for clarified instructions, or give up and report the failure. In the middle of a battle or a critical mission, this failure can cause confusion or threaten a mission's success, forcing the heroes to act quickly to resolve the situation. Gamemasters should take advantage of this potentially dramatic or amusing situation.

In adventuring parties featuring less than five players, adding a Gamemaster-controlled Droid to the party can be a great way to balance the party without having to adjust your encounter design. Many gaming groups can only muster three or four players and a Gamemaster, while the game is designed for a typical group of five players and a GM. Filling those empty player spots with Droids is a nice option, because a Droid can easily fade into the background during noncombat encounters, whereas a living character that does so might seem shallow and one-dimensional. If you decide to include a Droid ally as a means of filling out the party's numbers, you should probably think about the Droid just like you would any other major Gamemaster character, and plan on it being with the party for the duration of the campaign.

You should choose a [Droid Model](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid_Model "Droid Model") that fills an appropriate niche in the party. Similarly, you should probably think about the kinds of adventures your heroes are going to be going on, and what roles need to be filled. If the party lacks a [Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), a Pilot Droid seems like a good choice. However, there are other considerations. Some Droids don't do well on adventures due to their forms (try getting that [Wheeled](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wheeled "Wheeled") Droid up a long set of stairs). A good option for creating Droids to round out a party of heroes is to use a stock Droid Chassis and then build the Droid from the ground up. This not only lets you build exactly the kind of Droid you need to fill a gap in the party, it unshackles you from the usual physical forms of various Droids.

As the heroes gain levels, so should the Droid. The levels you choose for your Droid should likewise continue to help the Droid fill in the gaps in the party. If the party is short on firepower, the Droid should take levels with a high [Base Attack Bonus](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Base_Attack_Bonus "Base Attack Bonus") and good combat [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents"). However, you also have another option to consider. If you want the Droid to grow and to develop as an interesting Gamemaster character, consider having the Droid take levels in the [Independent Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Independent_Droid "Independent Droid") Prestige Class. This helps represent the fact that the Droid is becoming more and more unique thanks to its time with a party of heroes at the center of great, galaxy-shaking events.

For the most part, Gamemasters and players need not worry about the exact type of data ports and other connections between a Droid and a computer, [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle"), or network. However, the Gamemaster might decide that this information should play a part in the story or would otherwise logically come into play. For instance, it might make sense for an Astromech Droid to be able to slice into a complex computer system, but a lowly Power Droid probably does not have the equipment for it. Also, rare or alien Droids or those from another era might have nonstandard ports. Certain [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") and [Feats](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feats "Feats") require a link in order to function. Below is a brief summary of the standard data ports found in the galaxy.

**Maintenance Data Link:** All Droids come standard with at least one Maintenance Data Link port for programming and maintenance. This wired link can be used with [Datapads](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Datapads "Datapads"), [Portable Computers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Portable_Computers "Portable Computers"), and systems designed for implementing [Droid Modifications](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid_Modifications "Droid Modifications"). This link is not normally suitable for the Droid to use to attempt slicing or controlling a [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") or automated systems. Any attempt to do so suffers a -10 penalty to any [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer"), [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics"), or other [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks"). This penalty does not apply to [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") others make to the Droid.

**Basic Data Link:** Droids are equipped with one or more Basic Data Links. This wired link allows a Droid to transfer data efficiently and enables equipped Droids, such as Pilot Droids, to operate computers and ship systems without physically touching the manual controls, although that capability usually depends on the system rather than the Droid. The port is suitable for data transfer, vehicular control, and slicing attempts.

**[Scomp Link](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scomp_Link "Scomp Link"):** This advanced Droid-computer Data Link is found on [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") and on larger computers and networks. This port is suitable for all types of data transfer, vehicular control, and slicing attempts. All Astromech Droids are equipped with [Scomp Links](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scomp_Links "Scomp Links"), unless otherwise noted.

**Wireless Link:** Some Droids have the hardware to access Wireless Data Links. This type of link is only operable when a computer or network also has wireless capability. Accessing networks typically requires passwords or slicing. However, once connected, this type of link is suitable for all types of data transfer, vehicular control, and slicing attempts. An [Internal Comlink](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Internal_Comlink "Internal Comlink") does not allow a Droid to access wireless data networks or make slicing attempts wirelessly.

### Droid Quirks

Droids are temperamental machines, exposed to every kind of environmental and physical abuse. With limited programming and typically deficient maintenance schedules, many Droids develop odd mechanical glitches and personality quirks. Most are minor- more annoyances than anything dangerous. [Memory Wipes](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Memory_Wipes "Memory Wipes") can eliminate personality quirks, but they do nothing for physical abnormalities.

Droid Quirks are optional in *Saga Edition*. They make interesting and easy roleplaying hooks, as well as give players inspiration for the development of personalities and backgrounds for their Droids. Some Droid Quirks might also have minor mechanical effects. If no game mechanic is indicated in the quirk description, a +2 or -2 circumstance bonus to [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") or attack rolls might be appropriate whenever the quirk manifests. Players and Gamemasters are encouraged to select Droid Quirks that make characters memorable. Additionally, Droid Quirks can be assigned as the result of physical damage or sensory overload. If a Droid is reduced to 0 [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points"), roll 1d20+10. If the result exceeds the Droid's [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense"), the unit develops a Droid Quirk when it is repaired. The Gamemaster can assign an appropriate Droid Quirk or roll on Droid Quirks table.

Each Droid Quirk has an associated origin indicated in its description: Hardware (H) or Software (S). If both are indicated, one should be selected at the time the Droid Quirk is applied. Hardware Quirks result from faulty installations, physical damage, aging circuitry, or incompatible systems. Hardware Quirks can be removed by replacing or repairing the faulty system. Repairing Droid Quirks is notoriously difficult. If the repair check does not exceed the [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") DC by 10 or more, the new system works, but the Droid Quirk (or a similar Droid Quirk) returns 1d12 days later.

Software Quirks are tied to the Droid's internal subroutines. They can be caused by corrupted data, but they are more often a result of the Droid's learned responses from previous experience. Most Software Quirks are eliminated with a [Memory Wipe](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Memory_Wipe "Memory Wipe"). Corrupted data can be repaired with a successful DC 25 [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check to [Reprogram](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reprogram "Reprogram") the Droid. Attempting to eliminate a Software Quirk without a [Memory Wipe](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Memory_Wipe "Memory Wipe") is difficult, often requiring help. A DC 30 [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") check succeeds in eliminating a Software Quirk, but the Droid's personality and responses might change in unforeseen ways because the Droid must learn new responses. The results might not be exactly what was intended. Failure on the check results in the addition of another Software Quirk.

**Quirks as Persistent Conditions:** Another way to include Droid Quirks in the game is to apply one when a Droid suffers a [Persistent Condition](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Condition "Persistent Condition"). This allows the Gamemaster to use many Droid Quirks over the span of a campaign, and provides a rich source of plot hooks, humor, or dramatic situations. The Gamemaster should select a Droid Quirk that is related to the origin of the [Persistent Condition](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Condition "Persistent Condition"). For instance, a [Persistent Condition](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Condition "Persistent Condition") caused by physical damage should inflict a Hardware Quirk, whereas a hit from an [Ion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ion "Ion") weapon might cause either a Hardware or a Software Quirk. Droid Quirks applied in this manner should be automatically removed when the [Persistent Condition](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persistent_Condition "Persistent Condition") is removed, instead of requiring the skill checks normally needed to eliminate a Droid Quirk.

## Droids as Equipment

*Reference Book: [Scavenger's Guide to Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scavenger%27s_Guide_to_Droids "Scavenger's Guide to Droids")*

Few Droids are as special as [R2-D2](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/R2-D2 "R2-D2"). Countless Droids in the background of the *Star Wars* universe do their duty quietly and efficiently, allowing the heroes to have the spotlight. Droids can be useful, but having one as a full-fledged Gamemaster character can be distracting and time-consuming, both for players and for the Gamemaster. A hero who owns a [Starfighter](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starfighter "Starfighter") likely wants an Astromech Droid to make the calculations for hyperspace, but the hero probably wants the Droid to stay with the ship during adventures. The Gamemaster has enough to worry about without playing a precocious Astromech Droid, so the Droid is left out of the picture. But if the Gamemaster wants to run a scene in which the pilot rushes back to the hangar bay while pirates attempt to sabotage all the ships in the landing bay, that Droid becomes a significant part of the hero's attempt to stop the pirates. Additionally, consider the [Soldier](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Soldier "Soldier") hero who leads an entire squad of Battle Droids into combat. The addition of so many Gamemaster-character Droids can cause problems with encounter balance and slow down the action at the game table while the actions of all those Droids are resolved.

Ultimately, most Droids are better envisioned as equipment rather than as companions or allies. They are machines built to do a job. A personality might develop as a result of infrequent [Memory Wipes](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Memory_Wipes "Memory Wipes"), but the Droid is still hard-wired to do one particular task very well. This section explains how Droids can be treated as equipment rather than as Gamemaster characters, allowing Gamemasters to put control of those Droids into the hands of the heroes who own them.

These rules are not meant to replace the Droid rules in the *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*. They are optional and meant to be used in situations where a Droid is needed for utility and not as a significant Gamemaster character. Additionally, Gamemasters should use these rules when the heroes own a Droid that does not need to be a major part of the story. These rules allow players to purchase Droids to help them during their adventures without the Gamemaster worrying about players entering the fray with legions of Droids at their disposal

Under these rules, Droids are stripped down to their bare essentials, both in stats and in function. These functions are called protocols. A Droid is able to perform protocol actions only when told to do so by its master or its master's allies. Protocol actions are [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions"), but more complex ones require more than one [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action"). These [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") represent the hero giving orders to the Droid and the time required for the Droid to complete the action. Under these rules, each Droid has a number of protocols that it can complete on behalf of its master. Typically, these protocols are used during combat.

### Using the Protocol Format

Droids are normally unable to take protocol actions on their own. These actions come from a Droid's master and his or her allies. The Droid's owner, as well as anyone designated by that owner, can activate protocol actions for a Droid by spending, in the same turn, the number of [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") listed in the Protocol Format statistics block. However, you can spend [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") to activate a Droid's protocol actions only if no one has activated protocol actions for the Droid since the end of your last turn. A Droid carries out the protocol action immediately, as soon as the character spends the [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") or [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") to activate the protocol action. Some actions can be performed only once per turn, as indicated in the Protocol Format statistics block.

The Gamemaster gives the Droid's Protocol Format statistics block to the player, who then treats the Droid, effectively, as a source of extra actions. The Gamemaster should keep the Droid's full statistics block for occasions when information not found in the Protocol Format statistics block is needed. Other than execution of actions, the Droid follows all Droid rules and is subject to the same restrictions and benefits as those that normal Droids have.

Threshold Damage OnlyThreshold Damage Only

The protocol rules are meant as a replacement for the regular Droid rules, limiting the Droid's game options in order to speed play. Gamemasters looking to further streamline the Droid experience should consider the following optional damage rule.

When using the protocol rules, track only the Droid's condition modifier. If an attack successfully hits the Droid, it moves -1 step on the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"). If the damage total for the attack meets or exceeds the Droid's [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"), the Droid immediately moves -5 steps on the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track").

### How Does the Protocol System Work?

At first glance, players and Gamemasters might have difficulty understanding why these rules are in place or how they should be used. As an example, let's say that you have a [Twi'lek](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Twi%27lek "Twi'lek") [Scoundrel](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scoundrel "Scoundrel") hero named Reagle, specializing in slicing. Reagle wants to buy an Astromech Droid to help him slice into secure [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships"), but the Gamemaster does not want to develop a full personality and role in the story for the Droid. Rather than adding a new Astromech Gamemaster character to the game, the Gamemaster allows the player to control the Droid, using these rules. They give the player the Protocol Format statistics block for the Droid, and allows the player to spend [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") to control the Droid during encounters. The Gamemaster still keeps the full-length statistics block handy in case, for example, an enemy decides to shoot the Droid, but the Astromech is placed under Reagle's direct control.

### Adapting Droids to the Protocol Format

When using the Protocol Format, remember that the goal of these rules is to pare down the Droid to its essentials. If the Droid were only a tool or a piece of equipment, what would it do?

- **Droids can [Move](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move "Move").** Spend a [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") to command a Droid to move.
- **Droids can [Aid Another](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Aid_Another "Aid Another").** Spend a [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") for a Droid to provide a +2 bonus, assisting a character on an [Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Attack "Attack") or a [Skill Check](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Check "Skill Check").
- **Droids can make [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") of their own.** Spend a [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") for a Droid to make a [Skill Check](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Check "Skill Check"). If the check is considered a [Full-Round Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full-Round_Action "Full-Round Action"), this process costs two [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions").
- **Droids can [Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Attack "Attack").** Spend two [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") for a Droid to make an [Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Attack "Attack"). For a [Full Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full_Attack "Full Attack") Action, four [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") must be spent.

Every action that a Droid is able to perform might not be listed in the Protocol Format statistics block. Indicate the actions a Droid is most likely to be called on to perform. An action that the Droid will be expected to do every session or encounter should be included. A Droid should have no more than five protocols. An owner who wants a Droid with more functionality and flexibility should use the regular Droid rules and ask the Gamemaster to run the Droid as a Gamemaster character.

When converting actions, remember that protocol actions are [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions"). An action can require more than one [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action"), as indicated on table below. This conversion allows some actions to be performed more times per turn than the Droid would be able to do on its own. For example, a Droid might normally be able to take only two [Move Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move_Actions "Move Actions") on its turn; however, an owner using protocol actions can spend three [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") to order the Droid to move three times. This compromise accounts for the Droid taking up some of the owner's actions rather than having its own suite of actions.

| DROID ACTION COST | PLAYER ACTION COST |
| --- | --- |
| Free | 1 [Free Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Free_Action "Free Action") |
| Swift | 1 [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") |
| Swift x2 | 1 [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") |
| Swift x3 | 2 [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") (once per turn) |
| Move | 1 [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") (twice per turn) |
| Standard | 1 [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") (once per turn) |
| Full-Round | 2 [Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") (once per turn) |

Any action requiring more time than a [Full-Round Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full-Round_Action "Full-Round Action") is not suitable as a protocol action. Droids should be allowed to perform actions listed under the descriptions of [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") they are [Trained](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained "Trained") in. If a hero wants his or her Droid to perform an action outside the scope of its [Trained Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skills "Trained Skills") and protocols, the Gamemaster must determine if the Droid could reasonably perform the action and, if so, the action cost of that action.

#### An Example of Protocol Conversion

*Wren, a player in a campaign run by Brent, is playing an outlaw tech. After a combat encounter leaves her nearly dead, she tells Brent that she wants to invest in a Droid bodyguard for her character. The game has several players, and Brent does not want to divide the spotlight any further. He decides to use the protocol rules to give Wren more options in combat without having to worry about another Gamemaster character.*

*Wren wants her bodyguard to be an [HK-24 Assassin Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/HK-24_Assassin_Droid "HK-24 Assassin Droid") that her character repaired and restored from a junkyard planet. An HK Droid might seem a bit much for some Gamemasters, but Brent has some action-packed ideas about the Droid's previous owners and how those owners might make life interesting for his heroes. It is a win-win situation: Wren gets more options in a fight, and Brent gets some story options for exciting encounters*

*Brent agrees and pores over the stats of the [HK-24 Assassin Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/HK-24_Assassin_Droid "HK-24 Assassin Droid") in the* [Knights of the Old Republic Campaign Guide](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knights_of_the_Old_Republic_Campaign_Guide "Knights of the Old Republic Campaign Guide")*. He notes the [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") and [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold") of the Droid. The Droid movement is easy to convert. Because the Droid is meant to aid Wren's character in combat, Brent devotes a [Ranged Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ranged_Attack "Ranged Attack") action and an aid attack action to the Droid. He also gives the Droid an attack option since it possesses the [Rapid Shot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rapid_Shot "Rapid Shot") Feat. The Droid is [Trained](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained "Trained") in [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") and [Persuasion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persuasion "Persuasion"). The presence of on Assassin Droid can lend extra weight to a hero's [Persuasion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persuasion "Persuasion") attempt, so he gives the Droid an [Aid Another](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Aid_Another "Aid Another") action. The Droid has a [Translator Unit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Translator_Unit "Translator Unit"), so Brent gives the Droid the ability to translate as a [Free Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Free_Action "Free Action").*

## [Droid Allies](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid_Allies "Droid Allies")

*Reference Book: [Scavenger's Guide to Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scavenger%27s_Guide_to_Droids "Scavenger's Guide to Droids")*

Droids occupy a unique place in *Star Wars*. They are machines that feel, automatons that think, robots that know. They fight wars, heal the sick, and track down Rebel scum. A Droid develops a personality, friendships, and rivalries, just as an organic being does over the course of a lifetime. Droids might squabble among themselves, but they will also offer any parts necessary to help repair a friend fallen in battle. Organic beings often treat their Droids less like property and more like companions. Droids are a remarkable part of life, even if they are not truly alive.

Droids have a significant effect on history. Were it not for a pesky R2 unit, [The Rebel Alliance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Rebel_Alliance "The Rebel Alliance") would have never become [The New Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_New_Republic "The New Republic"). The legions of Battle Droids are worthy foes of [The Galactic Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Republic "The Galactic Republic"). A Droid's recording of a Jedi meditation leads [Zayne Carrick](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Zayne_Carrick "Zayne Carrick") to fight his former masters. Droids are important pieces to the puzzle of what the galaxy has become. Some Droid designations are well known as heroes or villains: [C-3PO](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/C-3PO "C-3PO"), [HK-47](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/HK-47 "HK-47"), and [IG-88](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/IG-88 "IG-88").

Yet, some beings in the *Star Wars* universe consider Droids to be nothing more than tools- just hydrospanners with legs. Droids are sent into battle to be destroyed. They are unmourned if they do not return from unexplored regions of space. Indeed, some beings resent Droids for their efficiency and cause of the perception that they take jobs from organic beings who need feed their families. Droids are refused service in some businesses, and they are denied basic rights on some planets. In spite of, or perhaps because of, all their amazing achievements, Droids can bring out the worst in other beings. Galactic conflicts, such as [The Clone Wars](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Clone_Wars "The Clone Wars"), in which Droids are programmed to kill and destroy are not forgotten by the beings whose homes and planets are overrun by mechanical menaces.

This chapter explores the role of Droids in the *Star Wars* universe, including their struggle for rights, their religious beliefs, and even a few old spacers' tales thrown in for good measure. It looks at Droids as equipment, as well as at some of the unique roleplaying opportunities available to Droid Heroes. Players who would like to give extra spark to their Droid Heroes can find plenty of interesting options. Gamemasters looking to add flavorful Droid characters to their stories can find useful hints, tips, hooks, and aids here.
