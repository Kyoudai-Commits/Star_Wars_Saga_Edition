# Destiny

> Source: [https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny) · Retrieved 2026-10-09T09:36:21Z.

*Reference Book: [Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*

Destiny plays a large role in the *Star Wars* universe. As a young boy, Anakin Skywalker is told that his Destiny is to bring balance to [The Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Force "The Force"). Padmé's Destiny is to give birth to the twins, Luke and Leia, so that they can fulfill their own Destinies. Luke Skywalker learns that his Destiny is to redeem his father, Darth Vader, so that balance to [The Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Force "The Force") is restored. Leia's Destiny is to save the Rebellion from annihilation at the hands of the Empire and help forge [The New Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_New_Republic "The New Republic"). The Destiny mechanic helps players and Gamemasters recognize that all heroes- and even major villains- have significant roles to play in the fate of the galaxy. Destiny rewards players for good roleplaying and gives Gamemasters new plot hooks to use when designing adventures.

The rules presented in this article are optional. A Gamemaster may decide to use Destiny Points or not; however, the decision to use them should be made before the campaign gets underway, so that each player can decide whether or not to embrace a Destiny for their character from the outset. Although conceivably any character- Heroic or Nonheroic- has a Destiny to fulfill, only Heroic characters receive Destiny Points and receive in-game benefits for pursuing their Destinies. Nonheroic characters don't receive Destiny Points; their Destinies, whatever they might be, exist purely on a story level.

## Destiny Points

Destiny Points are resources that a player can use to help fulfill whatever Destiny has been set before their character. A 1st-level character begins play with 1 Destiny Point and gains another Destiny Point at each level. A Destiny Point allows a hero to perform a nearly impossible task or survive against all odds. Only a character with a Destiny can gain or spend Destiny Points. A character may accumulate as many Destiny Points as their level. Thus, a 5th-level character may have as many as 5 Destiny Points if they don't spend any of them.

Spending a Destiny Point does not take an Action and grants one of the following benefits:

- Automatically score a [Critical Hit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Critical_Hit "Critical Hit") (no attack roll required).
- Automatically cause an attack made against you to miss (even after the attack is resolved).
- Act out of turn (thus changing your position in the [Initiative Order](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Order "Initiative Order")) once per encounter.
- Take damage that would otherwise harm another character within your [Reach](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reach "Reach").
- Increase the effect of some [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers") (as noted in their descriptions).
- Immediately gain 3 [Force Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Points "Force Points").

When a character fulfills their Destiny, they can choose to keep the Destiny Points that they accumulated, or transfer some or all of them to allied characters. A character who retains their unspent Destiny Points can't spend them until they acquire a new Destiny. A character who chooses to give away some or all of their Destiny Points can't give more than 1 Destiny Point to any single ally. Under no other circumstances may a character transfer their Destiny Points to another character.

For example, Sia-Lan fulfills her Destiny by redeeming a powerful darksider, after which she still has 2 unspent Destiny Points. Sia-Lan decides to give Vor'en and Rorworr- two of her compatriots- 1 Destiny Point each, leaving her with 0 Destiny Points.

Unless a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") or [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") is linked to a character's Destiny, a character cannot use a Destiny Point or any other ability (such as the [Jedi Master](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jedi_Master "Jedi Master") class feature [Serenity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Serenity "Serenity")) that automatically causes a specific outcome for an attack roll or skill check. Using this Force-based maneuver helps the character to let go of their conscious self (which is aware of and using the technology of the [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship")) and instead act on instinct, just as Luke did when he switched off his targeting computer during the Battle of [Yavin](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Yavin "Yavin").

## Choosing a Destiny

Players don't need to choose Destinies for their heroes at the start of the campaign- or ever, for that matter. Not every hero has a Destiny that must be fulfilled before the end of the campaign, and even players who want their characters to have Destinies need to give their characters- and the campaign- a chance to develop first. It may take several adventures before players understand where the campaign is going and what goals their heroes are likely to pursue.

Choosing a Destiny can be handled in one of two ways: either the player can select an appropriate Destiny for their hero (based on what's happening in the campaign), or the Gamemaster can select a secret Destiny for the hero. If the player chooses a Destiny for their character, the GM should challenge the player to fulfill their Destiny by presenting conflicting situations where the smartest and most beneficial decision might force the character to choose between doing what is best for their fellow heroes and taking steps toward their Destiny. If the GM secretly chooses the hero's Destiny, they must present challenges that let the character move closer to achieving their Destiny, imparting benefits when the character is moving in the right direction and imposing penalties when the character does something that takes them far from the Destined path. The GM-selected "Secret Destiny" forces the player to take their character through a period of self-discovery, as they learn which actions lead them closer to- or farther from- their Destiny. In essence, this mirrors Luke Skywalker's struggle at the end of *The Empire Strikes Back*, when he must choose between redeeming his father and falling prey to the corruption of [The Dark Side](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Dark_Side "The Dark Side").

A character with a Destiny gains short-term benefits whenever they make significant progress toward fulfilling it, while a character who pursues goals that move them further away form their Destiny suffers short-term negative effect. Conversely, a character without a Destiny has nothing to gain and nothing to lose.

### Sample Destinies

The following sample Destinies should provide a starting point for any character wishing to take advantage of the Destiny rules. Each Destiny includes a brief description, with examples.

- **Destiny Bonus:** When a character accomplishes a goal or performs a task that clearly moves them closer to fulfilling their Destiny (GM's determination), they gain this short-term benefit.
- **Destiny Penalty:** When a character does something that clearly moves them away from their Destiny (GM's determination), they suffer a short-term negative effect.
- **Destiny Fulfilled:** When a character fulfills their Destiny, they gain these permanent benefits. Sometimes fulfilling a Destiny has other effects as well; these are also covered here.

## Death and Destiny

Achieving one's Destiny may yield great benefits, but the path of Destiny can be perilous. Many characters in the *Star Wars* saga perish attempting to fulfill their Destinies. If a Force-sensitive character dies while fulfilling (or attempting to fulfill) their Destiny, the Gamemaster may allow the dead character to manifest as a [Force Spirit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Spirit "Force Spirit"). If a non-Force-user perishes while pursuing or achieving their Destiny, the GM may decide that the character's sacrifice or untimely death imparts some benefits upon their surviving allies.

### [Force Spirits](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Spirits "Force Spirits")

A Force-user who dies in the process of fulfilling their Destiny may manifest as a Force Spirit. For a Jedi or other good Force-users, this means transforming into a translucent blue spirit that can appear before their former allies. For a darksider, this means becoming an evil Dark Side Spirit that can linger on, continuing to spread the influence of [The Dark Side](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Dark_Side "The Dark Side").

If a character with the [Force Sensitivity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Sensitivity "Force Sensitivity") feat dies while fulfilling their Destiny, that character may become a Force Spirit, retaining their consciousness (and their [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence"), [Wisdom](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wisdom "Wisdom"), and [Charisma](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charisma "Charisma") scores) but becoming completely incorporeal. The Force Spirit can manifest at will and can vanish just as easily. It can also walk through walls and exist in the vacuum of space. Additionally, Force Spirits can travel anywhere in the galaxy instantly with a mere thought. However, Force Spirits have no substance and cannot interact physically with creatures or objects in the universe.

When a hero dies and becomes a Force Spirit, that Force Spirit becomes a GM-controlled character. Under the GM's control, a Force Spirit might serve as a guide, advising heroes in times of dire need and sharing valuable information or wisdom it held in life.

### Noble Sacrifice

Whenever a character willingly sacrifices themselves for a noble cause, particularly while fulfilling their Destiny, they can bolster the resolve of their surviving comrades and allies. For example, when Arvel Crynyd crashed his [A-Wing](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/A-Wing "A-Wing") into the bridge of The *Executor*, it was a turning point in the Battle of [Endor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Endor "Endor"). When a character dies fulfilling their Destiny in such a way, all allies within the same star system gain a +1 Destiny bonus on attack rolls and +1 Destiny bonus to their [Defenses](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Defenses "Defenses") ([Reflex](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex "Reflex"), [Fortitude](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude "Fortitude"), and [Will](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Will "Will")) for 24 hours.

### Vengeance

When a valid ally falls in the pursuit of their Destiny, it can have powerful effects on those present at the time of their death. When a character dies fulfilling their Destiny, any ally who witnesses their death may choose to become filled with a desire to avenge their fallen comrade, gaining a +2 Destiny bonus on attack rolls and damage rolls for 24 hours. Since revenge leads to [The Dark Side](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Dark_Side "The Dark Side"), any Force-user who chooses to gain these bonuses must immediately increase their [Dark Side Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dark_Side_Score "Dark Side Score") by 1.

## We've Been Through a Lot Together

*Reference Book: [Scum and Villainy](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scum_and_Villainy "Scum and Villainy")*

Sometimes, a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") is more than just a means of transportation. Sometimes, it's part of a character's Destiny. In fact, for some characters, it's the focal point of their Destiny, which might otherwise be nothing more than to wander aimlessly from one jam to another. No matter how humble (or downright ramshackle) a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") might appear, if it's the Destiny of a character to be linked with that ship, it can accomplish amazing things.

If you decide to link a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") (or, rarely, a [Planetary Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Planetary_Vehicle "Planetary Vehicle")) to your Destiny, that replaces the normal Destiny mechanic. In essence, you don't have a Destiny to accomplish any specific goal; instead, you're destined to have the most important events in your life with a specific [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"). This option is appropriate in campaigns with at least a moderate frequency of [Starship Scale](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Scale "Starship Scale") encounters, but it's certainly not restricted to such campaigns. In fact, if a campaign centers exclusively around [Starship Scale](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Scale "Starship Scale") action, this option might be too powerful as it allows you to access your Destiny Points in most encounters. Only the GM can decide if Starship-linked Destinies are appropriate for a given campaign.

You earn and spend Destiny Points with a Starship-linked Destiny using the same basic rules as with the normal Destiny options. You gain one Destiny Point at each level, and they never return once you spend them. However, having your Destiny linked to a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") causes the rules to work slightly differently.

First, if a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") is your Destiny, it is never completely destroyed. Even if it is blown up in battle or confiscated by [Hutts](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hutts "Hutts") before being crushed and melted, some small part of it survives. Perhaps you find the hyperspace core the whole [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") was built around, or its ruined hull is found drifting as debris after that battle. Your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") can cease to be functional as easily as anyone else's, but a recognizable part of it always survives for you to rebuild (or retrofit to a similar [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship")). It can be as time consuming and difficult as getting a new [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") would be (if not more so), but the result still qualifies as your Destiny-linked Starship.

Secondly, the options you can exercise by using a Destiny Point are slightly different, as detailed below. The following are possible only when you are acting as a crew member ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), [Copilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Copilot "Copilot"), [Gunner](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gunner "Gunner"), [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator"), [Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), or [Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer")) on the [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") linked to your Destiny:

- Automatically cause an attack rolled against your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to miss, even once the attack is resolved. ([Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), [Copilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Copilot "Copilot"), or [Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot") only)
- Automatically succeed on any [Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Pilot "Pilot") check, including any check to avoid a hazard or obstacle, getting the best possible outcome on the check. ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot") or [Copilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Copilot "Copilot") only)
- Allow your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to act out of turn, thus changing your position in the [Initiative Order](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Order "Initiative Order"). ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), [Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), or [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator") only)
- Cause any damage rolled against your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to instead affect any other [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") within three squares. ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), [Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), or [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator") only)
- Move your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to the top of its [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"). If any systems have been disabled because of being moved down your [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"), they now function. ([Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") only)
- Repair your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"), restoring a number of [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") equal to its [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"). ([Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") only)
- Restore your Starship's shields to full [Shield Rating](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Shield_Rating "Shield Rating"). ([Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") or [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator") only)
- If you are using [Starship Maneuvers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Maneuvers "Starship Maneuvers"), you can spend a Destiny Point to regain all uses of all your maneuvers. ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot") or [Gunner](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gunner "Gunner") only)

In general, a character with a Starship-linked Destiny can do what no one else can manage- unless they, too, are linked to its Destiny. There's nothing to prevent multiple characters from being Destiny-linked to the same [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"). Such characters might share the ship, or might constantly be stealing, buying, or gambling it back and forth between them.

## Heirloom Items

*Reference Books: [Legacy Era Campaign Guide](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Legacy_Era_Campaign_Guide "Legacy Era Campaign Guide"), [Web Enhancements](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Web_Enhancements "Web Enhancements")*

Over the course of galactic history, certain items, including weapons and armor, have been made famous by the person they belong to. The personal [Blaster Rifle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Blaster_Rifle "Blaster Rifle") of a hero of [The Mandalorian Wars](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian_Wars "The Mandalorian Wars") or a [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber") wielded by a dreaded [Sith Lord](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Sith_Lord "Sith Lord") is more than just another piece of [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"); it is a piece of history. It has become legendary because it is strongly associated with the power and destiny of its owner and is closely tied to that person's successes; for example, Ulic Qel-Droma's [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber") and Exar Kun's [Battle Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Battle_Armor "Battle Armor") were both integral in helping those characters fulfill their Destiny. Heirloom Items are exceedingly rare, and each is unique. Thus, they are highly sought-after by collectors and heroes alike.

An Heirloom Item carries a benefit that persists until the item's destruction. Such a benefit is the result of either the original owner's tinkering or a side effect of being present when a character's Destiny is fulfilled. The rules for Heirloom Items are optional. Any Gamemaster who adopts the Destiny system is free to include Heirloom Items in his or her campaign as well.

### Using Heirloom Items

In order for an Heirloom Item to come into being, the item must be present when a character fulfills his or her Destiny. When a character fulfills his or her Destiny, instead of gaining the "Destiny Fulfilled" effect of that Destiny, he or she can instead choose to create an Heirloom Item. That character then chooses one of the following effects, which is applied to a particular weapon, suit of armor, or item in the character's possession at the moment the Destiny is fulfilled. Typically this item is one of particular significance to the character.

Additionally, when a character with an unfulfilled destiny dies while in pursuit of his or her Destiny, instead of the normal effect ([Force Spirit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Spirit "Force Spirit"), Noble Sacrifice, or Vengeance) that character can choose to create an Heirloom Item instead. The possible Heirloom Item effects are discussed below:

- **Extra Modification:** The owner can modify the item a second time using the [Tech Specialist](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tech_Specialist "Tech Specialist") Feat.
- **Legendary Icon:** When carrying in plain sight, the owner gains favorable circumstances on all [Charisma](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charisma "Charisma")-based [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks") (except [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") checks).
- **Steeped in the Dark Side:** Once per encounter, while carrying or using the Heirloom Item, the owner can apply a Destiny bonus equal to one-half their [Dark Side Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dark_Side_Score "Dark Side Score") on any attack roll or [Skill Check](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Check "Skill Check") made before the end of their turn. Applying this bonus increases the owner's [Dark Side Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dark_Side_Score "Dark Side Score") by 1.
- **Symbol of the Light:** When carrying or using the Heirloom Item, once per encounter, as a [Reaction](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reaction "Reaction"), the owner can impose a penalty on any one attack roll or [Skill Check](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Check "Skill Check") made by a character with a [Dark Side Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dark_Side_Score "Dark Side Score") of 1 or higher. This penalty is equal to the target's [Dark Side Score](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dark_Side_Score "Dark Side Score").

Heirloom LightsabersHeirloom Lightsabers

A Force-sensitive character who acquires an Heirloom [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber") can attune himself or herself to the [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber"), gaining the benefits of the weapon as though he or she constructed the [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber") originally (see [Lightsaber Construction](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber_Construction "Lightsaber Construction")). The character in possession of the Heirloom Lightsaber must spend 24 hours meditating over the Weapon and spend a [Force Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Point "Force Point") to attune the [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber"). From that point on, the character is treated as the Lightsaber's builder and gains the normal bonus for being attuned to the [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber"); this can only be done with Heirloom Lightsabers, not other character's [Lightsabers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsabers "Lightsabers").
