# Abilities

> Source: [https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Abilities](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Abilities) · Retrieved 2026-10-09T09:36:19Z.

*Reference Book: [Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*

Just about every dice roll you make is going to get a bonus or penalty based on your character's Abilities. A tough character has a better chance of surviving a freezing night on [Hoth](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hoth?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hoth (page does not exist)"). A perceptive character is more likely to notice [Stormtroopers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stormtroopers "Stormtroopers") sneaking up from behind. A stupid character is less likely to find a concealed panel that leads to a secret cargo compartment. Your Ability Scores tell you what your modifiers are for rolls such as these.

Your character has six abilities: Strength (abbreviated Str), Dexterity (abbreviated Dex), Constitution (abbreviated Con), Intelligence (abbreviated Int), Wisdom (abbreviated Wis), and Charisma (abbreviated Cha). Each of your character's above-average Abilities gives you a benefit on certain die rolls, and each one below-average gives you a disadvantage on other die rolls. You roll your scores randomly, assign them to the Abilities you like, raise and lower them according to your character's [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species"), and then raise them as your character advances in experience.

## The Abilities

Each ability partially describes your character and affects some of your character's actions. The description of each ability includes a list of notable characters along with their scores in that ability.

Strength (Str) measures your character's muscle and physical power. This ability is especially important for [Soldiers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Soldiers "Soldiers") because it helps them prevail in physical combat.

You apply your character's Strength modifier to:

- Melee attack rolls.
- [Grapple](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grapple "Grapple") checks, if your Strength is higher than your Dexterity.
- Damage for Melee and Thrown Weapons. (*Exception:* [Grenades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grenades "Grenades") don't have their damage modified by Strength.)
  - When using a two-handed weapon, you add double your Strength bonus.
- [Climb](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Climb "Climb"), [Jump](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jump "Jump"), and [Swim](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swim "Swim") checks (the [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") with Strength as the key ability).
- Strength checks (for breaking down doors and the like).

Dexterity (Dex) measures hand-eye coordination, agility, reflexes, and balance. This ability is the most important ability for [Scoundrels](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scoundrels "Scoundrels"), but it's also high on the list for characters who want to be good shots with ranged weapons or who want to handle the controls of a [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") fairly well.

You apply your character's Dexterity modifier to:

- Ranged attack rolls, such as with blasters.
- Melee attack rolls, if you have the [Weapon Finesse](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapon_Finesse "Weapon Finesse") feat.
- [Grapple](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grapple "Grapple") checks, if your Dexterity is higher than your Strength.
- [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense"), provided the character can react to the attack.
- [Acrobatics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Acrobatics "Acrobatics"), [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative"), [Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Pilot "Pilot"), [Ride](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ride "Ride"), and [Stealth](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stealth "Stealth") checks (the [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") with Dexterity as the key ability).

Constitution (Con) represents your character's health and stamina. Constitution adds to a hero's [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points"), so it's important for everyone, but most important for [Soldiers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Soldiers "Soldiers") and [Jedi](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jedi "Jedi").

You may apply your Constitution modifier to:

- Each die roll for gaining additional [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") (though a penalty can never drop a roll below 1; a character always gains at least 1 Hit Point each time they go up a level).
- [Fortitude Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fortitude_Defense "Fortitude Defense"), for resisting [Poison](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Poison "Poison"), [Radiation](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Radiation "Radiation"), and similar threats.
- The [Endurance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Endurance "Endurance") skill.

Intelligence (Int) determines how well your character learns and reasons, Intelligence is important for [Scouts](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scouts "Scouts"), [Nobles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nobles "Nobles"), and any character who wants to have a wide assortment of [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills"). ([Beasts](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Beasts "Beasts") have Intelligence scores of 1 or 2. Sentient creatures have scores of at least 3.)

You apply your character's Intelligence modifier to:

- The number of [Languages](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Languages "Languages") your character knows at the start of the game, and the number of [Languages](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Languages "Languages") you learn when you take the [Linguist](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Linguist "Linguist") feat.
- The number of [Trained Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skills "Trained Skills") you have.
- [Knowledge](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge"), [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics"), and [Use Computer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_Computer "Use Computer") checks (the [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") with Intelligence as the key ability).

When a character's Intelligence score permanently increases or decreases, its number of [Trained Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skills "Trained Skills") and known [Languages](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Languages "Languages") also changes. For example, if Paul's [Wookiee](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wookiee "Wookiee") [Scout](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scout "Scout") increases his Intelligence from 13 to 14, his Int modifier goes from +1 to +2. Paul's [Wookiee](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wookiee "Wookiee") gains one new [Trained Skill](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Trained_Skill "Trained Skill") (chosen from his list of Class Skills) and one new [Language](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Language "Language").

The Wookiee's greater Intelligence allows him to utilize things he has learned but never before applied properly.

Every creature has a Wisdom (Wis) score. Wisdom describes a character's willpower, common sense, perception, and intuition. Compared to Intelligence, Wisdom is more related to being in tune with and aware of one's surroundings, while Intelligence represents one's ability to analyze information. An "absent-minded professor" has a low Wisdom score and a high Intelligence score. A simpleton with low Intelligence might nonetheless have great insight (high Wisdom). Wisdom is the most important ability for [Scouts](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scouts "Scouts"), but it's also important for characters wishing to be in-tune with their environment or characters who like to [Gamble](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gamble "Gamble").

If you want your character to have keen senses, put a high score in Wisdom.

You apply your Wisdom modifier to:

- [Will Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Will_Defense "Will Defense"), generally for resisting certain [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers")
- [Gambling](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gambling "Gambling") (excluding Pure Games of Chance)
- [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception"), [Survival](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Survival "Survival"), and [Treat Injury](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Treat_Injury "Treat Injury") checks (the [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") with Wisdom as the key ability).
- The number of [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers") you learn when you take the [Force Training](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Training "Force Training") feat.
- The number of [Starship Maneuvers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Maneuvers "Starship Maneuvers") you learn when you take the [Starship Tactics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Tactics "Starship Tactics") feat.

When a Force-Using character's Wisdom score permanently increases or decreases, their number of known [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers") also changes. For every instance of the [Force Training](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Training "Force Training") feat that you have, you gain one [Force Power](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Power "Force Power") (or an additional use of an already-known [Force Power](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Power "Force Power")) for every point by which your Wisdom modifier increases.

If you instead suffer a permanent reduction in your Wisdom modifier, you lose access to the same number of [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers"); you must choose which [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers") (or extra uses of the same [Force Power](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Power "Force Power")) you lose.

Every creature has a Charisma (Cha) score. Charisma measures a character's force of personality, persuasiveness, personal magnetism, ability to lead, and physical attractiveness. It represents actual strength of personality and force of presence, not merely how others perceive you in a social setting. Charisma is most important for [Nobles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nobles "Nobles") and [Jedi](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jedi "Jedi").

You apply your Charisma modifier to:

- [Deception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Deception "Deception"), [Gather Information](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gather_Information "Gather Information"), [Persuasion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persuasion "Persuasion"), and [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") checks (the [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") with Charisma as the key ability).

Intelligence, Wisdom, and CharismaIntelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma

A character with a high Intelligence score is curious, knowledgeable, and prone to using big words. A character with a high Intelligence but low Wisdom may be smart but absent-minded, or knowledgeable but lacking in common sense. A character with a high Intelligence but a low Charisma may be a know-it-all or a reclusive scholar. The smart character lacking in both Wisdom and Charisma usually puts their foot in their mouth. (A character with a low Intelligence mispronounces and misuses words, has trouble following directions, or fails to get the joke.)

A character with a high Wisdom score may be sensible, serene, "in tune," alert, or centered. A character with a high Wisdom but a low Intelligence may be aware, but simple. A character with a high Wisdom but a low Charisma knows enough to speak carefully and may become an adviser or "power behind the throne" rather than a leader. (A character with a low Wisdom score may be rash, imprudent, irresponsible, or "out of it.")

A character with a high Charisma may be beautiful, handsome, striking, personable, and confident. A character with a high Charisma but a low Intelligence can usually pass themselves off as knowledgeable, until they meet a true expert. A charismatic character with a low Wisdom may be popular, but they don't know who their real friends are. (A character with a low Charisma may be reserved, gruff, rude, fawning, or simply nondescript.)

### Ability Modifiers

Each ability, after changes made because of [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species"), has between a -5 to a +5 modifier. The table below shows the modifier for each ability, based on its score.

The modifier is the number you add to or subtract from the die roll when your character tries to do something related to that ability. For instance, you add or subtract your Strength modifier to your roll when you try to hit someone with a [Vibroblade](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vibroblade "Vibroblade"). You also apply the modifier to some numbers that aren't die rolls, such as when you add or subtract your Dexterity modifier to your [Reflex Defense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reflex_Defense "Reflex Defense"). A positive modifier is called a bonus and a negative modifier is called a penalty.

### Ability Checks

Sometimes you try to do something to which no specific [Skill](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill "Skill") applies. In these cases, you make an Ability Check- roll 1d20 and apply the appropriate Ability modifier. The GM assigns a DC, or sets up an opposed check when two characters are engaged in a contest using one Ability against another. The character who rolls highest acts first.

In some cases, a test of one's Ability doesn't involve luck. Just as you wouldn't make a height check to see who is taller, you don't make a Strength check to see who is stronger. When two characters arm wrestle, for example, the stronger character simply wins. In the cases of identical scores, then make opposed Strength checks.

| EXAMPLE ABILITY CHECK | KEY ABILITY |
| --- | --- |
| Forcing open a jammed or locked door | Strength |
| Tying a rope | Dexterity |
| Holding one's breath | Constitution |
| Navigating a maze | Intelligence |
| Remembering to lock a door | Wisdom |
| Getting yourself noticed in a crowd | Charisma |

## Ability Generation

To create an Ability Score for your character, roll four six-sided dice (4d6). Disregard the lowest die and total the three highest dice.

This roll gives you a number between 3 (horrible) and 18 (tremendous). The average ability score for the typical galactic citizen is 10 or 11, but your character is not typical. The most common ability scores for player characters (heroes) are 12 and 13. (The average hero is above average.)

Make this roll six times (five times if playing as a [Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid "Droid")), recording the result each time on a piece of paper. Once you have all six scores, assign each score to one of your six Abilities. At this step, you need to know what kind of person your character is going to be, including their [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") and [Class](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Class "Class"), in order to know where best to place your character's ability scores. Remember that choosing a [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") other than [Human](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Human "Human") causes some of these ability scores to change.

If your scores are too low, you may scrap them and reroll all six scores. Your scores are considered too low if your total modifiers (before changes according to [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species")) are 0 or less, or if your highest score is 13 or lower.

### Planned Generation

Instead of rolling dice, you may select the scores you want by using the planned character generation method. This requires a bit more thought and effort on your part, since you need to know what kind of character you want to play so you can select your scores as outlined below.

Your character's scores all start at 8. You typically have 25 points to spend to increase these scores (21 points if playing as a [Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid "Droid")), using the costs shown below. After you select your scores, apply any [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") modifiers. In the *[Dawn of Defiance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Dawn_of_Defiance "Dawn of Defiance")* campaign, you build a character with 28 points (23 points if playing as a [Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid "Droid")).

### Standard Package

The third method of determining ability scores is the standard score package, a balanced mix of scores designed to quickly create hero characters. Assign the scores to the abilities you like. After you assign your scores, apply [Species](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Species "Species") modifiers (if playing a [Droid](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droid "Droid"), use the first 5 standard scores).

The standard score package is:

15, 14, 13, 12, 10, and 8.
