# Rank and Privilege

> Source: [https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rank_and_Privilege](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rank_and_Privilege) · Retrieved 2026-10-09T09:36:24Z.

*Reference Book: [Galaxy at War](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Galaxy_at_War "Galaxy at War")*

A hero who serves in an army, a planetary defense organization, or a mercenary group is given a rank here called a title. With a title comes authority to give orders, access to equipment and personnel, and increased pay. However, the greater a hero's title is, the greater are the accountability, responsibilities, and demands placed on him or her. The *Star Wars Roleplaying Game* is about action, however, not administrative paperwork or boards of inquiry, so heroes in the game are allowed more leeway than actual members of the military would ever hope for. A title is usually indicative of Character Level and experience, but a title can be based on social status and other noncombat-oriented factors. Heroes often receive orders from officers who are superior in rank but considerably lower in level- sometimes having only [Nonheroic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nonheroic "Nonheroic") levels.

The Rank and Privilege system is based on and is compatible with the [Organizations](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Organizations "Organizations") described in the *[Force Unleashed Campaign Guide](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Unleashed_Campaign_Guide "Force Unleashed Campaign Guide")*, and it also stands on its own as an independent optional system. Gamemasters using both these systems should determine the scale, organization type, and positive and negative criteria for specific military organizations in their campaigns.

## Organization Scores

The Rank and Privilege system provided here gives a character's title and the corresponding additional in-game abilities and bonus for requisitioning equipment (see Gear Requisition, below). As a player advances in title, he or she also gains access to additional benefits. With title also comes authority, and as such, heroes add the appropriate bonus to any [Persuasion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persuasion "Persuasion") checks when dealing with members of their military organization (see "Bonus" column in table below).

Military organizations follow a strict chain of command, with clear regulations on rank, responsibility, and authority of each soldier in its command. Soldiers are expected to follow the orders without question, and officers expect that their orders are carried out quickly and efficiently by those under their command.

Noncommissioned OfficersNoncommissioned Officers

In real-world military units, there is a difference between officers who go through years of elite officer training and those enlisted men and women who are promoted to leadership roles. Officers who are enlisted are called Noncommissioned Officers, and they are drawn from the ranks of occupational specialists within the enlisted personnel in a military force. Enlisted personnel sometimes then go on to attend officer candidacy schools (or receive such training during active duty, in the case of militaries that have no formal academies).

In the *Star Wars* setting, there are often few, if any, differences between commissioned and noncommissioned officers. Some organizations, such as [The Rebel Alliance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Rebel_Alliance "The Rebel Alliance"), have no choice but to draw upon members of their own organization for officers. As such, almost every officer in [The Rebel Alliance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Rebel_Alliance "The Rebel Alliance") is what would normally be considered a noncommissioned officer; typically, these officers begin their careers as specialists within a Rebel cell, and advance based on merit. In [The Rebel Alliance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Rebel_Alliance "The Rebel Alliance") (and similar organizations), advancement is so fast that there can be no practical difference between commissioned and noncommissioned officer. Heroes like [Han Solo](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Han_Solo "Han Solo") and [Lando Calrissian](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lando_Calrissian "Lando Calrissian") reach the rank of General quickly, out of necessity.

If your heroes are members of a military unit that does have a strong divide between commissioned and noncommissioned officers (such as [The Galactic Empire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Empire "The Galactic Empire")), there are a few adaptations you can make to the rules to enforce this divide.

- First, Noncommissioned Officers have a maximum Organization Score of 11, meaning that no noncommissioned officer can increase his or her rank beyond Master Warrant Officer unless that character then goes on to receive [Officer Candidacy Training](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Officer_Candidacy_Training "Officer Candidacy Training").
- To determine if a hero is a noncommissioned or a commissioned officer, look at how that hero came to be involved with the military unit.
  - If the hero was drafted into service, signed on to the unit with a specialty other than leadership, or signed on with no prior training or specialty, that hero is probably a noncommissioned, enlisted soldier.
  - If the hero attended a military academy (such as the one at [Carida](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Carida?action=edit&redlink=1 "Carida (page does not exist)")), received formal military training as a part of his or her upbringing (as is the case for many [Nobles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Nobles "Nobles")), or signed on with a unit specifically for a leadership role, chances are that hero is a commissioned officer.

Additionally, for military units where there is a distinction between commissioned and noncommissioned officers, the ranks for those heroes with an Organization Score of 11 or less should be different for commissioned officers. Though the actual ranks vary from one military outfit to the next, the following basic ranks can be used for commissioned Officers progressing through the first few ranks of the command hierarchy: Ensign, Sub-Lieutenant, Second Lieutenant.

## Rank and the Officer Prestige Class

When using the Rank and Privilege system, a character can take the [Officer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Officer "Officer") Prestige Class without having a sufficient Organization Score to put him or her in the officer rank category (Score 12; Second Lieutenant). Such a character might have the knowledge, experience, and command ability to place him or her in the ranks of officer at a later date. Many noncommissioned officers, such as Warrant Officers, are capable of commanding more troops than their rank implies, but choose to remain in the field with their soldiers, thriving on the challenges that come with being in close proximity with the troops that they lead or taking on specialized tasks without the burden of command.

Alternatively, heroes who take levels in the [Officer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Officer "Officer") Prestige Class can be automatically raised to the rank of Second Lieutenant and can be assumed to have the required Organization Score.

## Special Orders

As a hero advances in rank, he or she can give Special Orders. Special Orders provide a once-per-mission effect, such as calling in an air strike, radioing back to headquarters for technical information, or requesting immediate evacuation from a battlefield. To use a Special Order, the highest-ranking character in the adventuring group must be able to communicate with his or her superiors, either by [Comlink](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Comlink "Comlink") or by messenger. A Special Order can have immediate results, or it might take some time and require additional prerequisites. Also, in some cases, Special Orders cannot be enacted, especially if the hero is far from other troops or is involved in a clandestine mission without the benefit of additional forces.

Special Orders are divided into Minor Orders, Major Orders, and Command Orders. Several examples of Special Orders are described below, and Gamemasters are encouraged to create their own or to determine if a special order described by a player might be within the hero's authority.

Multiple Heroes with RankMultiple Heroes with Rank

Not all of your heroes have to be members of a military unit to go on missions on behalf of those units. However, if you have multiple heroes who all have ranks in the same military outfit, there may be some question as to who the commander is on a particular mission. There are several ways to determine who acts as the commander on a particular mission; note that only the commander of a mission can issue Special Orders during that mission. Below are some suggestions for determining the commander when multiple heroes hold rank in the same military unit.

- **The hero with the highest Organization Score is the commander.** For this method, simply compare the heroes' Organization Scores in order to determine who has the highest; that person becomes the commander for the mission. In case of a tie, a commander is assigned by the organization's higher authorities based on the mission profile. This method ensures that the hero with the highest rank is always the commander of the mission, and rewards heroes who specifically take levels in certain classes (particularly the [Officer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Officer "Officer")) and choose to act as the party's leader.

- **The heroes rotate as commander based on specialty.** A somewhat more egalitarian method, in this scenario the mission commander is chosen by the heroes superiors based on his or her specialty.
  - For example, on a mission to locate Count Dooku's secret lair, the heroes' superiors might put the [Jedi](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jedi "Jedi") character in charge, owing to their familiarity with [The Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Force "The Force"), [The Jedi](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Jedi "The Jedi"), and [The Sith](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Sith "The Sith").
  - If the mission calls for extended travel through the wilds of [Daluuj](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Daluuj "Daluuj"), the [Scout](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scout "Scout") character could be put in charge thanks to their record of survivability.
  - For missions where combat seems to be most likely, the [Soldier](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Soldier "Soldier") character might be put in charge.
  - Provided that the missions the heroes undertake have some variety in their goals and methods of execution, each of the heroes should get a chance to take the lead on certain missions. This method works out very well when the heroes are all members of the same military unit and all have some rank.

- **The heroes choose a commander from among themselves.** Under this option, the heroes receive a mission from their superiors and are then required to choose one of themselves to be the commander. This not only allows the heroes to spread around the chance to be a commander from mission to mission, it also allows heroes who don't want a position of leadership to opt-out easily while still participating in the choice of a commander.

## Gear Requisition

Training, equipping, and arming a soldier in a professional army costs tens of thousands of credits. Soldiers rarely carry their personal gear and weapons into battle and instead rely on their military units to outfit them with the gear necessary for battle. In addition to three square meals a day and a cot, one advantage to serving in a regular army or mercenary unit is access to [Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapons "Weapons"), [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"), and [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") that are beyond the reaches of civilians. A well-equipped and well-funded army or mercenary outfit is assumed to have gear, [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles"), and [Droids](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Droids "Droids") appropriate to their faction and era. In most cases, the average soldier has access to gear and equipment far beyond what would normally be available to a character by using the wealth system in the *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*.

### Using Gear Requisition

Gear Requisition is a set of optional rules that replaces that standard system of wealth system described in the *[Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*. The Gear Requisition system gives players in a [Military Campaign](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Military_Campaign "Military Campaign") access to bigger and better [Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapons "Weapons"), [Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armor "Armor"), [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles"), and even [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") that would exceed the limits of their personal wealth. However, the equipment that the heroes requisition does not belong to them and must be returned to the army or mercenary company's quartermaster at the end of the adventure. When the heroes are off duty or not otherwise working on behalf of their units, they must rely on their own weapons and gear to survive.

Requisitioned Gear is distinct from gear assigned for a specific mission. Requisitioned Gear can be used in any way that the hero sees fit, just like personal gear. Heroes who requisition gear for a mission might receive additional unique, mission-specific items or [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") that would otherwise exceed their requisition budget. However, gear assigned for a mission must be used in accordance with the mission's parameters. A soldier is both responsible and accountable for gear assigned as part of a mission, and reckless endangerment or destruction of this equipment could result in harsh discipline. For example, the heroes might be ordered to transport an experimental weapon to the battlefield, but the weapon is not theirs to deploy unless given direct permission. Also, [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") and [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") that ferry the heroes to and from locations and do not otherwise accompany the heroes after drop off are not considered part of a requisition budget.

If you utilize this system in your campaign, then the heroes should receive only one-half the usual rewards in credits that they would otherwise gain (see [Other Rewards](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Other_Rewards "Other Rewards")). Heroes in a military-themed campaign are fighting for the prestige and glory, not the credits, and if they choose to leave their military organization, they discover that life as a civilian comes without access to the best gear, weapons, and gadgets that soldiers enjoy.

### Requisition Limits

To determine the amount of credits that the heroes have available to spend at the beginning of an adventure, use the following formula:

**Requisition Amount = Total number of Character Levels x 2,000 credits**

For example, the heroes are four 4th level characters- privates or troopers with no bonus to requisitioning (see Rank and Requisition, below). The 16 total Character Levels multiplied by 2,000 equals a total of 32,000 credits available to spend on [Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapons "Weapons"), [Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armor "Armor"), [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"), and [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") for the duration of the mission.

Heroes can spend up to the allotted amount of credits on any [Weapons](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Weapons "Weapons"), [Armor](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Armor "Armor"), [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"), or [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") (but not [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships"); see Requisitioning Starships, below) that the Gamemaster allows. Any credits not spent are lost- heroes should maximize their purchases to get as much equipment as possible. Military organizations have enormous budgets, contracting standards, and [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") pools, so heroes pay the [Used](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Used "Used") cost for [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") when requisitioning. [The Galactic Republic](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Republic "The Galactic Republic") and [The Galactic Empire](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Galactic_Empire "The Galactic Empire") are wealthy and powerful enough to issue every Clone Trooper and Stormtrooper the appropriate armor, so Clone Trooper or Stormtrooper heroes can ignore the cost of armor when using the Gear Requisition system.

### Rank and Requisition

Heroes gain rank as they advance within a military organization. With this increase in rank comes greater spending limits for requisitioning gear. The Organization Scores table indicates the bonus that heroes receive when making a requisition request. This bonus is applied only to the highest-ranking hero's Character Level, essentially treating the hero as that many levels higher when requisitioning gear.

For example, a 6th-level character who has obtained the rank of Second Lieutenant (+4 bonus) is treated as 10th level when requisitioning gear. The other three characters in this adventuring group are equal in Character Level but lower in rank, so they do not receive a bonus. The total Character Levels of one 10th level character and three 6th level characters is 28, which when multiplied by 2,000 gives a total of 56,000 credits available to that group for requisitioning equipment.

### Requisitioning Starships

Instead of requisitioning extensive collections of personal equipment, heroes might prefer to spend their requisition allotment to obtain a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") or two for their mission. However, the prices of most [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships"), even at the [Used](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Used "Used") price, are beyond the reach of all but the highest-level heroes, so players who decide to requisition [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") use the following formulae instead of the regular requisition amount:

**Requisition Amount for Personal Equipment = Total number of Character Levels x 500 credits**

**Requisition Amount for Starships = Total number of Character Levels x 10,000 credits**

For example, four 10th level heroes receive a requisition limit of 20,000 credits for personal equipment and gear, and they receive an additional requisition allotment of 400,000 credits for any [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") available to their faction and era.

To determine the price of a requisitioned [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"), use the Starship's [Used](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Used "Used") price. Extra credits not spent on [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") cannot go towards personal gear and vice versa. Allotted credits not spent toward their requisitioning limit for purchasing [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") are lost for that adventure.

A requisitioned [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") comes with any crew members required to operate the vessel, unless the heroes decide to fill the positions themselves. The captain of the [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") might be of higher rank than the heroes, but he or she receives orders to follow the heroes' instructions.

Gamemasters should remind players that not every mission requires the use of a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"). Heroes in a campaign focusing on gritty house-to-house combat might find a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to be unnecessary and better small arms, armor, and [Grenades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grenades "Grenades") more appropriate. Heroes are not required to requisition a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") merely for transportation if that [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") does not otherwise accompany them on their mission. Heroes who requisition a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") can operate it as they please, as if it were their personal property, but they still must return the [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to the quartermaster at the end of the mission.
